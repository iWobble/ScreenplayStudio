# Running the pipeline in Marvin

When the story lives in a Marvin workspace (my-studio on your-marvin.example.com), **the records are the source of truth**, not `stories/<slug>/` files. The file route in pipeline.md is the fallback for when no workspace is reachable. Reach the workspace with the CLI: `marvin --workspace my-studio platform ...`. Image and voice generation run through Marvin's integrations (OpenAI Images, ElevenLabs), each behind an approval Jared gives in Marvin. This replaces "Claude writes, Jared generates" for stills and voice.

## Which record holds what

| Record (entry type) | Holds | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| `world` | style rules for every picture | one place; never repeated into prompts by hand |
| `story` | premise, `world`, `script` (markdown), format, status | `status`: script, voices, rough-cut, final, published |
| `actor` | a person in the world's troupe: the look that stays the same in every role (`base` woman or man, build, hair, skin, a general-view picture, a default voice). Belongs to the **world** | not costume or props: a peg leg is a prop of the character, the actor has both legs |
| `cast-member` (shown as **Character**) | an actor playing a role in **one story**: `story`, `actor`, `role`, `presence` (`on screen`, or `voice only` for someone heard and never seen, like the narrator: no picture needed), `speaker` (the name its speech lines use, e.g. `captain`: the script breakdown joins lines to characters by it), `costume`, `props`, a voice override, and the approved master picture | a master must be approved before a still uses it; the same actor can be a different character in the next story |
| `casting` | the search for a role: a candidate `actor` for a `character`, `fit` (look, feel, voice), a `screen_test` picture, `status` (considering, auditioned, chosen, passed) | the chosen casting sets the character's `actor` |
| `scene` | place, light, set dressing, continuity, `anchor` picture, ambience (and whether it is muffled) | every beat in the scene inherits it; put recurring props (the letters on the table) here, not in each beat |
| `beat` | one moment: shot, cast in frame, `expression`, `focus` prop, `references`, `lead_seconds`/`gap_seconds`/`tail_seconds`, `sounds` | status queued, made, approved |
| `still` | the request and the picture made from a beat | |
| `speech-line` | what is spoken (`text` is the source; `direction`, `delivery_text`, `caption`, `stability`, `similarity_boost`, `style`, `speed`, `take_notes`), its voice, its takes | the script and beat sheet are copies of this |
| `voice` | an ElevenLabs voice id: an **actor's** voice (listed in the Troupe, titled `Sarah's voice`), which a character uses unless it names another | free tier cannot use library voices; premade ones work |
| `sound` | a reusable sound (`kind` effect or ambience, `source`, optional `story`), tagged | **library item**: any story's beat or scene may name it by slug |
| `job`, `job-kind`, `worker` | heavy work queued for a worker (the audio cut), what runs it, who is alive | see Workers |

There is no entry-to-entry reference field: a child holds its parent's **slug** as text (a list as comma text or JSON). Check the slug before writing it.

## Stage by stage

1. **Brainstorm and script**: create or update the `world` and `story` records, not just `worlds/<slug>.md`.
2. **Beats**: one `beat` record each, with its `scene`, cast IDs and states, and the previous beat's outcome. Scarce props still never replenish.
3. **Stills**: **do not hand-write a still prompt.** A prompt written by hand misses the letters, the daylight, the continuity (this happened on beat 04). The still workflows compose it from the world, scene, beat and cast records, and attach the references in order: scene `anchor`, cast masters, beat `references`. To change a picture, change the record (an `expression` that is too soft, a missing prop on the scene) and redo, not the prompt. Judge every picture by eye against the masters and the scene before approving. A master's smile survives a prompt asking for another face, so state a flat expression on the beat and expect to need a better reference.
4. **Lines and sounds**: set a `speech-line` or `sound` to status `queued`. That runs `screenplay-take-line` or `screenplay-take-sound`: one spend approval, generate, attach the take, then keep or redo. **Redo sets status back to queued** and runs only that item; approved ones are untouched. `storyworld-record-lines` does the next 25 lines under one approval. Listen before approving; an approval plays the take.
5. **Listen**: the `screenplay-audio-cut` workflow creates a `job`; a worker stitches narration, sounds and the scene ambience (loud outside, low-passed and quiet inside, continuous across beats) into one cut with a timeline. Jared listens, names a line, and the fix goes into that `speech-line` or `beat`, then a new cut.
6. **Shots, render, publish**: assembly and the YouTube upload stay outside Marvin's records for now (`render/`). **Never publish to YouTube without Jared's say-so; uploads are private only.**

## Workers

Heavy jobs run on a generic worker (`InnerOpen/marvin-worker`): a standing runner that registers the kinds it can do, or a dispatcher that starts one per job. Kinds declare what they take, give and need, and a bad job fails at once with the reason. This repo's `render/` registers `audio-cut`. Settings -> Workers shows who is alive, what each kind takes, the queue, and each job's log and result.

## Rules that cost us time

- One approval covers every paid call after it in a run; a repeat cannot hold its own approval, and repeats take at most 25 items. Word the approval with line and character counts, and check usage on the free ElevenLabs tier (10,000 characters per period).
- Say "no alphabet characters, signs or captions" in a picture rule, never "no letters": letters are props.
- After any upload, check the stored `mimeType`; a call can succeed and store the wrong type.
- Library first: before making a sound, scene or voice, look for one that already exists (tags say what it is, collections group packs).

## The package

All of this (the 17 entry types, the 12 `screenplay-*` workflows and the 7 collections) is one Marvin **package**, `screenplay-studio` (Screenplay Studio): the pipeline, not any one world. In my-studio it shows as one collapsed "Screenplay Studio" section on the Workflows page and under Automation -> Packages. `docs/screenplay-studio.marvin-package.json` is the same package as a file. A world (Storyworld is the first; another could be Farm Friends) is records inside it: a `world` entry, its actors, its stories. To use it in another workspace: Automation -> Packages -> Add a package, choose that file, read the preview, install, then "Turn on workflows" (they arrive off, and need the `elevenlabs` and `openai_images` integrations, which they name). Installing never overwrites. After changing a workflow or record type here, rebuild the file from Automation -> Packages (build from this workspace, pick the same items, "group them here") and commit it, so the file and the live package do not drift.

The three record types workers use (`job`, `job-kind`, `worker`) are core's "Workers" blueprints now (Settings -> Workers -> Set up workers, or `marvin platform workers setup`); this package also carries them, so it installs on its own and says "exists" for any that core already added.

**Direction on a speech line.** `direction` is how a line should sound and feel, in plain words ("tired, a little amused, almost to herself"). The voice cannot read it: Claude turns it into the voice settings (`stability` lower is more expressive, `style`, `speed`) and, for the v3 model, into `delivery_text`, the same words with tags like `[sighs]`, `[whispers]`, `[pause]`. A line with `delivery_text` is recorded by `storyworld-take-line-tagged` (model `eleven_v3`, asks the same approval); one without is recorded by `storyworld-take-line`. Captions always use `text`, never `delivery_text`. `take_notes` stays the record of what sounded wrong and what was changed (a take workflow also writes a line there when it records). Every recording is kept as a **`take` record** (its audio, the settings and text it was made with, and your notes on it), because Marvin keeps a line's `audio` field and its attachment in step, so an older recording would otherwise be detached when the field changes. The take with Status `in_use` is the one the line uses (`audio` and `duration_seconds` on the line follow it); set another take's Status to `in_use` and save to switch (the workflow `storyworld-use-take`; a new recording becomes in use and the previous one goes back to `recorded` via `storyworld-take-created`). Punctuation changes delivery: a full stop, comma or ellipsis in `text` or `delivery_text` shifts the pacing and weight, so try the line with and without before reaching for tags. Check one short line before a whole story: v3's accepted settings and the caption word timings against tagged text are not yet confirmed.

**Captions follow the voice.** A recording keeps its own word timings in its asset metadata (`words`: when each word is spoken; Marvin keeps them from a speech step's answer), and the Audio cut and the render time captions from the recording the line uses, so choosing a different take (Use as Audio) brings that take's timings. A caption override (`caption` different from `text`) gets none and is spread by length; a v3 tag like `[sighs]` is never shown. Recordings made before this have no timings and fall back to the even spread: record the line again. The line's own `words` field is not used (it would belong to whichever take was newest).

**The script.** The `script` job kind (queue a `job` with `kind: script`, `args: {story: <slug>}`) makes the story's screenplay from its records and keeps four files on the story: `script_pdf` (Courier 12, the one to share or print), `script_fountain` (plain Fountain text for a screenwriting app), `script_html` and `script_breakdown` (who speaks and who is on screen, with lines, words, seconds and beats; each location and its beats; each sound cue: what a casting or recording pass starts from). A `scene` becomes a numbered scene heading (numbered by its first beat), the scene's place and look the first action line, a beat's `picture` an action line headed by the beat number, a speech line a character cue with its text, the narrator `NARRATOR (V.O.)`, a line's `direction` its parenthetical, and `sound` placements `SFX 1.6s: ...` cues (an ambience is named when it changes). Make it again after the records change; the new files replace the old (which go to the Trash). Beat numbers are the story's own: add a beat between 04 and 05 as `04a`, not by renumbering, so scene numbers and takes keep pointing at the same beat. Needs `screenplain` in the worker image (pinned in the Dockerfile).

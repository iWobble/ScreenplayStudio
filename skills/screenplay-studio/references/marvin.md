# Running the pipeline in Marvin

When the story lives in a Marvin workspace (for example a workspace called `my-studio`), **the records are the source of truth**, not `stories/<slug>/` files. A world's own pipeline skill (Storyworld's, for example) can describe a file-based fallback for when no workspace is reachable. Reach the workspace with the CLI: `marvin --workspace <workspace> platform ...`. Image and voice generation run through Marvin's integrations (OpenAI Images, ElevenLabs), each behind an approval the author gives in Marvin. This replaces "Claude writes, the author generates" for stills and voice.

## Which record holds what

| Record (entry type) | Holds | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| `world` | what never changes between its stories: the style `rules` for every picture, and its `bases` (the general figures actors are made from) | one place; never repeated into prompts by hand. Where and when a story is set, and its mood pictures, are **not** here: they change from story to story and live on the story |
| `story` | premise, `setting` (where and when: era, places, kind of people), `world`, `script` (markdown), `references` (mood and location pictures for this story), format, status, and for an episode its `series` and `episode` number | `status`: script, voices, rough-cut, final, published |
| `series` | a set of stories (episodes) that share a `world`, a cast and a `premise`; `status` planning, running, finished | an episode is a `story` that names its series (slug) and episode number; the cast (actors) is the world's, each episode makes its own characters |
| `chapter` | one chapter of a long story: `story`, `number` (zero-padded, in play order), a title, a `summary` | each `beat` names its `chapter` (slug). Slug `<story>-chapter-<nn>`. A story that is not cut into chapters has none |
| `actor` | a person in the world's troupe: the look that stays the same in every role (`base` woman or man, build, hair, skin, `distinguishing_features`, `baseline_wardrobe`, a `general_view` picture, a default voice and a `voice_sample` to hear). Name the picture file for the actor (`Nell.png`): "Create entry from asset" takes the name and slug from it. Belongs to the **world** | not costume or props: a peg leg is a prop of the character, the actor has both legs |
| `cast-member` (shown as **Character**) | an actor playing a role in **one story**, at one `age_state` (young, mid or old; the `screenplay-make-master` workflow makes its master from the actor's views tagged for that age): `story`, `actor`, `role`, `presence` (`on screen`, or `voice only` for someone heard and never seen, like the narrator: no picture needed), `speaker` (the name its speech lines use, e.g. `captain`: the script breakdown joins lines to characters by it), `costume`, `props`, a voice override and its voice sample (both filled from the actor when the actor is set, if the character has no voice of its own; a different voice or sample is left alone), and the approved master picture | a master must be approved before a still uses it; the same actor can be a different character in the next story |
| `casting` | the search for a role: a candidate `actor` for a `character`, `fit` (look, feel, voice), a `screen_test` picture, `status` (considering, auditioned, chosen, passed) | the chosen casting sets the character's `actor` |
| `scene` | place, light, set dressing, continuity, `anchor` picture, ambience (and whether it is muffled) | every beat in the scene inherits it; put recurring props (the letters on the table) here, not in each beat |
| `beat` | one moment: shot, cast in frame, `expression`, `focus` prop, `references`, `lead_seconds`/`gap_seconds`/`tail_seconds`, `sounds` | status queued, made, approved |
| `still` | the request and the picture made from a beat | one still can serve several beats: give each its own `move` (`{"from":{"x","y","zoom"},"to":{...}}`, x and y 0-1 across the picture, zoom 1 or more) so each looks at a different part of it, e.g. a push in on a face for a close-up. Reuse before generating |
| `speech-line` | what is spoken (`text` is the source; `direction`, `delivery_text`, `caption`, `stability`, `similarity_boost`, `style`, `speed`, `take_notes`), its voice, its takes | the script and beat sheet are copies of this |
| `voice` | a voice the troupe can speak with: its ElevenLabs id (optional until it is cloned), kind (`category`), a `preview` clip to hear, and its labels as **tags** (male, british, deep...). The **Voice library** lists them all; an actor's `voice`, a character's override and a line's `voice` pick one from it (v54: these fields carry `ref: "voice"`, so forms show a pick list, not a slug box; a character's `actor` picks from the world's troupe the same way). A voice is added from an mp3 or a recording (its `preview`) or by the **`screenplay-import-voices`** workflow (a job of kind `import-voices`), which creates only the new ones on the workspace's ElevenLabs key, matched by voice id (library voices need a paid plan) | one per ElevenLabs voice; a story's own record (`the-last-onion-voice-cook`) also carries the `speaker` its lines use |
| `sound` | a reusable sound: `kind` (**effect**: placed at a moment in a beat; **ambience**: a bed that loops under a scene; **music**: a bed under the whole story), `prompt`, `source`, `duration_seconds`, `loop`, `volume`, ElevenLabs's `prompt_influence` (0 to 1: how literally it follows the description) and `model`, `fade_in` / `fade_out` seconds, optional `story`; tagged | **library item**: any story's beat or scene may name it by slug. Finer types (the place, the material, the action, the era) are **tags**, because ElevenLabs has no sound categories and the mix only treats the three kinds differently |
| `job`, `job-kind`, `worker` | heavy work queued for a worker (the audio cut), what runs it, who is alive | see Workers |

There is no entry-to-entry reference field: a child holds its parent's **slug** as text (a list as comma text or JSON). Check the slug before writing it.

## Stage by stage

1. **Brainstorm and script**: create or update the `world` and `story` records, not just `worlds/<slug>.md`.
1b. **From a Fountain to records (`import-beats`, the *Narrate a story* guided setup)**: the story is one Fountain document, written by you or by an AI, in the story's *Fountain* field (or uploaded as its *Fountain file*). Run the *Narrate a story* guided setup, or the `screenplay-import-screenplay` workflow with Preview set to yes first: it says what it would make and makes nothing. The import makes the chapters, beats, spoken lines, characters, scenes and sounds to fill in (below), and a re-run adds what is missing and leaves what exists alone (Refresh also brings in line the beats and lines nothing has been made from, and moves what is gone from the Fountain to the Trash when nothing was made from it). Each run appends a dated line to the story's `sheet_log`.

**How the Fountain is cut (no AI, no cost: the same Fountain always gives the same chapters and beats).** Where a chapter begins is the writer's to say with Fountain's own `#` section lines (`# Act One`, `## The heist`; a number at the start of a title is the chapter's number); with none, whole scenes are grouped into chapters of about 12 pages. A beat is one picture: a scene heading with its first action, then each further action paragraph, with the dialogue after it as its lines; a **page break** (`===`) always starts the next picture, so two small actions can be kept as two pictures. A note on a beat (`[[Hold: 6s.]]`) sets how long its picture stays when nobody speaks (the *Complete* script export writes it). Speaker names become one-word handles (`OLD COOK` is Cook). Beats are numbered chapter x 1000 + n (a one-chapter story is plain 01, 02...), so adding to one chapter never renumbers another. **Check the split first** (preview): the plan and what a run did are written to the story's `split_plan`, and a round-trip check refuses a cut that would lose a spoken line or an action paragraph. **Bring in a piece at a time** with `part` (`3`, `2-4`, `1,3,5`; or the story's `import_part`): what is in other chapters is never touched, even in refresh mode.

2. **Beats**: one `beat` record each, with its `scene`, cast IDs and states, and the previous beat's outcome. Scarce props still never replenish.
3. **Stills**: **do not hand-write a still prompt.** A prompt written by hand misses the letters, the daylight, the continuity (this happened on beat 04). The still workflows compose it from the world, scene, beat and cast records, and attach the references in order: scene `anchor`, cast masters, beat `references`. To change a picture, change the record (an `expression` that is too soft, a missing prop on the scene) and redo, not the prompt. Judge every picture by eye against the masters and the scene before approving. A master's smile survives a prompt asking for another face, so state a flat expression on the beat and expect to need a better reference.
4. **Lines and sounds**: set a `speech-line` or `sound` to status `queued`. That runs `screenplay-take-line` or `screenplay-take-sound`: one spend approval, generate, attach the take, then keep or redo. **Redo sets status back to queued** and runs only that item; approved ones are untouched. `storyworld-record-lines` does the next 25 lines under one approval. Listen before approving; an approval plays the take.
2b. **Chapters and episodes** (a long story, or a series): cut a story longer than a few minutes into `chapter` records and put each beat in one (`beat.chapter`); the chapters play in `number` order and a chapter's beats stay together. The filmstrip labels each shot with its chapter, and the `audio-cut` job returns `result.chapters`: the "0:00 Title" lines for the video description (YouTube turns them into chapter markers only when there are at least three, the first at 0:00, each at least ten seconds; the job's log says which rule a story breaks). One video is one `story`; a second video in the same world with the same recurring cast is another `story` whose `series` names a `series` record and whose `episode` is its number (not a chapter of the first). Shorts need neither.
5. **Listen**: the `screenplay-audio-cut` workflow creates a `job`; a worker stitches narration, sounds and the scene ambience (loud outside, low-passed and quiet inside, continuous across beats) into one cut with a timeline. the author listens, names a line, and the fix goes into that `speech-line` or `beat`, then a new cut.
5b. **Filmstrip (the approval before a render)**: queue a `job` with `kind: filmstrip` (or run `screenplay-filmstrip`). It makes one page that plays the story's sound (lines, sound effects and the scene ambience, the same mix the render will use) while it shows each beat's still moved by its `move`, with captions and a strip to jump along; it is kept on the story as `filmstrip` (open it in its own tab with Open Raw: the preview frame blocks scripts). `screenplay-filmstrip-done` then raises an approval; **Approve for rendering** marks every beat of the story approved. A beat with no audio still needs `hold_seconds` to be in the film (the closing smile).
6. **Shots, render, publish**: assembly and the YouTube upload stay outside Marvin's records for now (`render/`). **Never publish to YouTube without the author's say-so; uploads are private only.**

## Workers

Heavy jobs run on a generic worker (`InnerOpen/marvin-worker`): a standing runner that registers the kinds it can do, or a dispatcher that starts one per job. Kinds declare what they take, give and need, and a bad job fails at once with the reason. This repo's `render/` registers `audio-cut` and the other media kinds. `convert-audio` (workflow `screenplay-convert-audio`, inputs entry, field, format) turns a recording a browser made (webm, ogg) into an mp3 (default) or m4a that plays anywhere: it reads the entry's audio field, converts, and points the field at the new asset; the original stays in the library and a file already in that format is left alone. Settings -> Workers shows who is alive, what each kind takes, the queue, and each job's log and result.

## Rules that cost us time

- One approval covers every paid call after it in a run; a repeat cannot hold its own approval, and repeats take at most 25 items. Word the approval with line and character counts, and check usage on the free ElevenLabs tier (10,000 characters per period).
- Say "no alphabet characters, signs or captions" in a picture rule, never "no letters": letters are props.
- After any upload, check the stored `mimeType`; a call can succeed and store the wrong type.
- Library first: before making a sound, scene or voice, look for one that already exists (tags say what it is, collections group packs).

## The package

All of this (the 19 entry types, the 20 `screenplay-*` workflows and the 8 collections) is one Marvin **package**, `screenplay-studio` (Screenplay Studio): the pipeline, not any one world. In a workspace it shows as one collapsed "Screenplay Studio" section on the Workflows page and under Automation -> Packages. `docs/screenplay-studio.marvin-package.json` is the same package as a file. A world (Storyworld is the first; another could be Farm Friends) is records inside it: a `world` entry, its actors, its stories. To use it in another workspace: Automation -> Packages -> Add a package, choose that file, read the preview, install, then "Turn on workflows" (they arrive off, and need the `elevenlabs` and `openai_images` integrations, which they name). Installing never overwrites. After changing a workflow or record type here, rebuild the file from Automation -> Packages (build from this workspace, pick the same items, "group them here") and commit it, so the file and the live package do not drift.

The package also brings two guided setups (Workspace -> Guided setups) and the **Timeline** view `story-timeline` (v51). **Open call** (`open-call`, v52) is the casting director's first job, before any story: scout the troupe of a world (add an actor, upload a headshot), take an actor's headshot (`general_view`) and fill in their sheet, then the **Voice library** (v54: a gallery of the voices with a play button; add one from an mp3, a recording, or *Import from ElevenLabs*) and give each actor a default voice from it; its end checks say which actors still lack a headshot or a voice. **Narrate a story** (`narrate-a-story`) then casts a story from that troupe: its Characters step is where a role gets its actor, speaker word and age; the troupe step was moved out of it in v52. Per-role auditions (`casting` records: considering, auditioned, chosen, passed) and the masters stay as they are. A story's page has a Timeline button next to *View on site*, and the guided setup's last button opens the story there: the beats as a filmstrip, each as wide as it will last in the render (lead + lines + gaps + tail, or `hold_seconds` when a beat has no lines), chapters over them, timecodes under, the lines and placed sounds in lanes. Two more lanes (v53): **Ambience**, one block per run of beats whose scene names the same `ambience` sound (the scene's level, a beat's own `ambience_volume` over it, "muffled" shown), and **Music**, the story's `music` sound under everything. Play layers sound effects and beds over the lines at their levels, once they have a take; a bed with none is drawn dotted. A line with no take yet is drawn hatched and measured from its words at 150 a minute, so the timeline is useful before anything is recorded; Play walks the takes there are in the browser, no cut job needed. It reads what is there and changes nothing: fix a beat or a line on its own page (Open on the card).

The three record types workers use (`job`, `job-kind`, `worker`) are core's "Workers" blueprints now (Settings -> Workers -> Set up workers, or `marvin platform workers setup`); this package also carries them, so it installs on its own and says "exists" for any that core already added.

**Direction on a speech line.** `direction` is how a line should sound and feel, in plain words ("tired, a little amused, almost to herself"). The voice cannot read it: Claude turns it into the voice settings (`stability` lower is more expressive, `style`, `speed`) and, for the v3 model, into `delivery_text`, the same words with tags like `[sighs]`, `[whispers]`, `[pause]`. A line with `delivery_text` is recorded by `storyworld-take-line-tagged` (model `eleven_v3`, asks the same approval); one without is recorded by `storyworld-take-line`. Captions always use `text`, never `delivery_text`. `take_notes` stays the record of what sounded wrong and what was changed (a take workflow also writes a line there when it records). Every recording is kept as a **`take` record** (its audio, the settings and text it was made with, and your notes on it), because Marvin keeps a line's `audio` field and its attachment in step, so an older recording would otherwise be detached when the field changes. The take with Status `in_use` is the one the line uses (`audio` and `duration_seconds` on the line follow it); set another take's Status to `in_use` and save to switch (the workflow `storyworld-use-take`; a new recording becomes in use and the previous one goes back to `recorded` via `storyworld-take-created`). Punctuation changes delivery: a full stop, comma or ellipsis in `text` or `delivery_text` shifts the pacing and weight, so try the line with and without before reaching for tags. Check one short line before a whole story: v3's accepted settings and the caption word timings against tagged text are not yet confirmed.

**Captions follow the voice.** A recording keeps its own word timings in its asset metadata (`words`: when each word is spoken; Marvin keeps them from a speech step's answer), and the Audio cut and the render time captions from the recording the line uses, so choosing a different take (Use as Audio) brings that take's timings. A caption override (`caption` different from `text`) gets none and is spread by length; a v3 tag like `[sighs]` is never shown. Recordings made before this have no timings and fall back to the even spread: record the line again. The line's own `words` field is not used (it would belong to whichever take was newest).

**The script.** The `script` job kind (queue a `job` with `kind: script`, `args: {story: <slug>}`) makes the story's screenplay from its records and keeps four files on the story: `script_pdf` (Courier 12, the one to share or print), `script_fountain` (plain Fountain text for a screenwriting app), `script_html` and `script_breakdown` (who speaks and who is on screen, with lines, words, seconds and beats; each location and its beats; each sound cue: what a casting or recording pass starts from). A `scene` becomes a numbered scene heading (numbered by its first beat), the scene's place and look the first action line, a beat's `picture` an action line headed by the beat number, a speech line a character cue with its text, the narrator `NARRATOR (V.O.)`, a line's `direction` its parenthetical, and `sound` placements `SFX 1.6s: ...` cues (an ambience is named when it changes). Make it again after the records change; the new files replace the old (which go to the Trash). Beat numbers are the story's own: add a beat between 04 and 05 as `04a`, not by renumbering, so scene numbers and takes keep pointing at the same beat. Needs `screenplain` in the worker image (pinned in the Dockerfile).

**Transcribing a recording (a start for a new script).** The `transcribe` job kind (run it with the `screenplay-transcribe` workflow, or queue a `job` with `kind: transcribe`, `args: {audio: <asset slug or id>, hotwords: "names to listen for", language: "en"}`) turns an uploaded audio or video asset into text with faster-whisper, free, in its own container (`transcribe/`, worker `desktop-transcriber`). It keeps the words, the words with times, the words in paragraphs and JSON as assets; open the paragraphs file to read it or cut it into beats.

**The video (`render`).** The `render` job kind makes the story's video from its approved stills and recorded lines (1080x1920, captions burned in) and keeps it on the story as `video`; the previous copy goes to the Trash. Approving the filmstrip (`screenplay-filmstrip-done`: *Approve for rendering*) queues it, and the job itself marks every beat approved first (a workflow step can only loop 25 items, so a long story's beats are approved by the job). Run it by hand with the `screenplay-render` workflow. By default a 720x1280 copy goes to Marvin (its asset limit is 100 MB); the full-quality file is kept in the worker's output folder (`STUDIO_OUT_DIR`, `~/screenplay-studio-out`); `quality: full` uploads the full file when it fits. Workers: one image, `screenplay-studio-worker:v<package version>`, run as two containers (docker/README.md).

**Overlay, music level and ambience (v81).** A video can sit over the still: set `overlay` (a video asset), `overlay_blend` and `overlay_opacity` on the **story** (the whole film), a **scene** (its beats) or a **beat**; the beat's wins over the scene's over the story's, and the blend `none` at a lower level turns an inherited overlay off. The render loops it to the shot's length, fitted to cover the frame. An MP4 has no transparency: `screen` drops black (rain, sparks), `multiply` drops white, `normal` with opacity fades it in; a WebM (VP9 with alpha) keeps real transparency with `normal`. A beat's `music_volume` replaces the story's music level while it plays (0 is silence; the music eases over half a second), and the story's `music_ducking` (`light` or `strong`) lowers the music by a set amount (about 9 dB or 18 dB) under each spoken line, whatever the voice's loudness, easing over 0.2 s down and 0.5 s back; lines closer than 0.7 s apart count as one (v85; before that it was a compressor keyed by the voice, so the dip depended on how loud the narration was and was often hard to hear). The story's `ambience` (with `ambience_volume`) is a bed under the whole film, and it plays **together with** the ambience of a beat or its scene (v92: before that a beat's or scene's bed replaced it): a beat's own `ambience` (a sound slug) takes the place of its scene's for that beat, and the two layers add. A bed's level order is the beat's `ambience_volume`, then the scene's (or, for the story's bed, `ambience_volume` on the story), then the sound's own, then 0.5. The Timeline's Ambience lane shows every layer, stacked on rows when they overlap, and a **Scene band** over the beats wraps the beats that share a scene (a beat's Scene and Chapter are pick lists that offer only the scenes and chapters of the beat's own story, narrowed as you set its Story; one already chosen from another story stays, marked, so a save never drops it). The Timeline draws them (v82): an **Overlay** lane (the video's file name, its blend and strength, and whether it came from the beat, the scene or the story; a blend of `none` leaves a gap), and the **Music** block shows a beat's `music_volume` as steps, a silent beat hatched; Play follows the steps. Levels are drawn as the render plays them (v83): the level set where a bed is named replaces the sound's own, and a sound with none plays music at 0.12 and ambience at 0.5. **Fades (v84).** Each bed block on the Timeline slopes where it fades: in and out over the sound's own `fade_in` / `fade_out`, else the render's defaults (an ambience crossfades over 0.6 s where the place changes; the music fades out over 2 s), and the block that runs to the end of the film over the story's **`end_fade_seconds`** when that is set (it covers the music and the last ambience at once). The lines lane's small 0.3 and 0.35 numbers are not fades but the quiet lead-in before the first line and the tail after the last. **Play follows the render (v86):** the timeline's Play button fades each bed in and out over its block (and over the story's `end_fade_seconds`) and dips the music under each spoken line by the story's `music_ducking`, with the render's amounts, so what you hear on the timeline is what the render will do (before this, Play stopped a bed dead and never ducked). Pieces with no take are silent in Play, so nothing dips for them. **Lengths of old recordings (v90):** the render, the filmstrip and the audio cut now measure a take whose header has no length (a browser recording made before lengths were measured, saved as .mp3) by listening to it, instead of stopping with "has no duration". **A beat's status `queued`** starts *take a still for a beat* (an OpenAI image, with an approval first), which needs the beat's scene, cast and reference pictures; if it cannot run, the beat goes back to `draft` rather than staying queued. To render a beat, use its *Draft* button on the Timeline, not its status. **Pictures, GIFs and fades over a still (v101):** an overlay may now be a PNG with transparency (a framed text card), an animated GIF (typed-out words: it plays once and holds its last frame) or a video (it loops); *Overlay: loop or play once* changes that, *Overlay fade* fades it in and out, and *Overlay start delay* holds it back for a moment. All three are set at beat, scene or story level and the beat's wins. A GIF's transparency is on or off per pixel, so for smooth edges use a WebM with transparency or a PNG. **The older script path is retired (v117).** A story's text is its Fountain and nothing else: the *Script*, *Screenplay* and *Beat sheet* fields, the `import-beats` beat-sheet reader, the `sync-sheet` job and the workflows *import a story's beats*, *rewrite a story's script from its beats* and *cut a screenplay into a beat sheet* are gone. The four stories that still had text in them (One Small Boat, The Last Onion, Granny Advice, Test Story) were each converted to a Fountain that holds every detail their records had, and the old texts are kept in `~/.config/screenplay-studio/legacy-scripts/` on the worker's machine. **Script with *version: complete*** makes that Fountain from any story's records at any time: the title page (credits, premise, setting, format, length, music), each character as a note with role, costume, props, rules and the actor, each scene as a heading (numbered) with its place, light, set dressing, continuity and ambience, each beat as the picture with a note of its hold, focus, expression, camera move, cast and sounds, each spoken line as dialogue with its direction and voice, and a page break between beats; it reads back as the same beats and lines. **Revising a story (v123).** Edit the story's Fountain (add narration, add dialogue, reword, move or remove a scene) and run the import again with *Refresh* (the *Narrate a story* guided setup's Check the split step previews it first and writes the result into the story's *Split plan*). The new text is fitted to the beats already made **by what they say, not by where they stand**: a beat whose words are unchanged keeps its number, picture, recordings and sounds; a beat reworded keeps its number and is updated if nothing was made from it, or kept and flagged if it has a picture or a recording; a beat that is new is numbered after the beat before it (`1002a`, `1002b`; at the start of a chapter `1000a`), so nothing after it is renumbered and nothing already made is lost; a beat gone from the text is moved to the Trash if nothing was made from it, and kept and flagged if something was. The preview says how many beats are unchanged, reworded, new (with their numbers) and gone. New spoken lines are made as drafts to record, new beats have no picture yet, and every beat and line keeps its own piece of the Fountain up to date. This needs every beat of the story to hold its own piece of the text (the import made since v114 does; an older story is matched by number until it has been brought in again). A speech by a narrator or announcer makes a *voice only* Character (no master picture). Page breaks (`===`) are the way to keep an added picture its own beat. **Revision drafts (v124).** To try a rewrite before it touches the story, make a *Revision* (a record with the proposed Fountain, pasted or attached, and a note). Run *Screenplay: check a revision*: it changes nothing and fills the Revision's report: beats unchanged, reworded, new (with their numbers) and gone, the beats still to make and the lines still to record. When the report is what you want, run *Screenplay: apply a revision*, which asks for your approval, then puts the proposed text into the story, refreshes the beats as above and marks the Revision applied; if the story's text changed since the check, it refuses and asks for a new check. A failed apply puts the story's text back. The story then carries the Revision's number and date (`revision`, `revision_date`), and the printed script shows them on its title page (the draft date line), and the breakdown lists the applied Revisions. **A beat's cast and reference pictures (v125).** The import fills a beat's *Cast* with the Characters in its picture (those who speak in it, then those its action names; never a narrator or announcer) and its *Reference pictures* with their master pictures, so a beat is ready for a still without hand work. It fills only what is empty: a cast you set stays, reference pictures you set (a picture of the place) stay and the masters follow them. A Character with no master yet joins the cast only; run the import again (Add) once the master is approved and the pictures are filled in. The check says how many records it would fill. **Redo (v125).** Redo on a still, a sound, a piece of music or a take sets the record to `redo`; the workflow “Screenplay: start a Redo again” puts it back to queued two or three minutes later (a workflow cannot start itself, so a new run is started from a different one), and the first workflow asks to spend again. Change the status in that time to cancel it. **Replace a story from a revision (v126).** For a rewrite where little of the old text is left, set the revision's *How to bring it in* to `replace` before checking it: the check says what goes to the Trash (beats, lines, takes, sounds made for single beats and chapters; they are renamed `…-before-revN` first, because a trashed record still holds its slug), and applying it starts the story afresh from the revision with beats numbered from 1001. Characters, scenes, ambience beds, the score, masters and voices stay. Every applied revision keeps the text it replaced in *The text it replaced*, so going back is a new revision holding that text. **Setting, premise and what is still missing (v127).** The import fills the story's *Setting* and *Premise* from `Setting:` and `Premise:` lines on the title page when the fields are empty (one that is filled in stays). With no `Setting:` line the setting is the first sentence of the first synopsis (`= …` under a scene heading), else the first place and its time of day; the premise is never made up. Every import and every check ends by naming what the story still lacks of World, Setting and Premise, which the picture workflows read. A character introduced as `CHET, 20s, clutches a logbook` is read like `CHET (20s)`. To write a Fountain that the import reads completely, read references/fountain.md. **From import to finished film (v122), what is made by hand and what by workflow.** *Actor sheet*: the workflow “Screenplay: make an actor's sheet” makes an actor's four-view sheet from the actor's fields (the world's base sheet and `style_actor`'s sheet are the references, so the faces keep the house style: dot eyes, tiny smile, flat matte forms; the picture is portrait, so the four views are laid out 2 by 2). *Cover*: “Screenplay: make a story's cover” makes the vertical movie poster (title in gold on a dark painted block) from the story's `leads` (their slugs, most important first) with an existing cover as the style to copy; the story needs a Setting and a Premise. A picture that is used only as a style reference must show no people, or the prompt must say to copy the style only: otherwise its characters appear in the new picture. *Stills*: a beat needs its Scene, its `cast` (Character slugs), `references` (their master pictures; for a beat with nobody in it, a people-free picture of the story such as its first still), `expression` and `focus`, and the story needs a `world` and a `setting`; say in `expression` what a character has lost (a hat, a cutlass), because nothing carries it from beat to beat. A beat with no cast now makes a still too. **A beat with a picture always plays** in the render, at its hold (else 4 seconds, the length the Timeline draws), whether or not it has words or sound; a beat with no words, no sound, no hold and no picture is left out and the render's log names it. **Guided setups after the import (v116):** *Narrate a story* now ends on what the import made: the *Scenes* step is for checking the scenes it made from the headings, *Beats and their scenes* for moving a beat or changing its hold, and a new *Sounds to fill in* step lists the draft Sounds (open one to upload or generate it, delete what you do not want). Its *Characters* step is gone: the characters the import made are the *Casting call*'s first step (*The roles*), so casting starts from them instead of asking for roles again. The review lists the characters and scenes the import made. **Everything the Fountain implies is made (v115):** bringing a Fountain in also makes a **Character** for each speaker, a **Scene** for each place and a **Sound** for each sound it calls for, so there is nothing to type before casting. A Character has the name the screenplay gives where it introduces them (`CAPTAIN VALERIE "THE VULTURE" VANE (40s) stands…` makes *Captain Valerie "The Vulture" Vane*), and its *Role* holds the sex (only when the pronouns or a word like boy or woman say it), the age and the introducing paragraph as written; a speaker who is never introduced still gets a Character with only the name. Costume and props are not guessed from the prose: they are left empty for you, the Casting call or the master-prompt step. A Scene is a place once, however many times and at whatever time of day the headings name it (`INT. CAPTAIN'S CABIN - NIGHT` and `- DAWN` are one Scene): *Place* says the name and inside or outside, *Look* the times of day, an interior is *muffled*, and each new beat is put in its Scene. **Sounds to fill in:** a note that says `[[Sound: a pot lid clanks at 1.5s.]]` is the writer's own cue and becomes a draft **Sound** placed on that beat at that moment (the *Complete* script export writes every placed sound this way). What a beat's own words call for becomes one too, placed on that beat, so it shows in the Timeline's Sounds lane as a block you can open to upload a recording or ask for one to be generated: sung words (`~` lyrics) as a music Sound (its prompt is the words as written, with the sentence that sets them up), `the sound of X`, `a splashing sound` and a loud word in capitals (`a deafening CRUNCH`) as effects (the prompt is the sentence they came from), and the weather and water a place's action describes (rain, thunder, waves, wind) as an ambience bed, which the Scene names as its *Ambience*. A Sound is made as a draft with nothing queued, so nothing is generated or paid for until you ask; a screenplay that says nothing of a sound gets none, and the story gets one **score placeholder** (a draft music Sound named for the story, set as the story's *Music* when that is empty, with the chapters' synopses as its prompt) because a screenplay almost never says what the music is: replace it with a recording or a generated take, or delete the Sound if there is no score (then clear *Music* on the story). Names, places and the scene heading are never read as sounds. Running the import again on a story made earlier fills in the Scenes and Sounds it lacks, puts the beats that are in no Scene or have no sounds into theirs, and gives every beat and spoken line made earlier its own piece of the Fountain (with its transition and the dual-dialogue flag), so a story made before the Fountain can be exported *as written*; it never changes a field that is set. The Timeline draws each picture in the story's shape (*Picture shape*, `aspect`: a Short tall, a wide video wide). Nothing is cast or dressed: no actor is set. A Character, Scene or Sound that is already there is left as it is, so running the import again changes nothing you have edited. **Nothing the writer wrote is dropped (v114):** each beat and each speech keeps its own piece of the Fountain, exactly as written, in its own *Fountain* field: its scene heading, section line, synopsis, notes, boneyard, lyrics, emphasis marks, transition and scene number. The story keeps the title page as fields (*Credits*: credit, author, source, draft date, contact, copyright, and the other lines) and the front matter as written; a transition before a beat also sets its *Transition into this beat* (a cut, a fade or a dissolve); a synopsis fills the chapter's summary; a speech written as dual dialogue (`^`) is marked *spoken at the same time as the line before*. The picture text, the spoken words and the rest are made from them, the way they were before. **Script export, as written:** *Script* with *version: written* writes the Fountain back from those pieces, with the title page first and the same words in the same order (only blank lines can differ); it needs a story made from Fountain, and says so when it is not. **Fountain is the one source (v113):** a story is one Fountain document, written by you (or by an AI), and nothing else is typed in as a “script” or a “screenplay” any more. Put it in the story's **Fountain** field, or upload a `.fountain` file as its **Fountain file** (for a long script; the worker reads a file of any size a file can be uploaded at, up to 20 MB). Narrate a story (the old *Bring in a screenplay* is part of it now) checks the split with no AI and no cost, then brings it in: a chapter for each `#` section line (with none, scene breaks about 12 pages apart), a beat for each picture, a spoken line for each speech, and **each chapter keeps its own part of the Fountain text, as written (notes and all), in its own Fountain field**, so a feature-length script is never one huge field. A story of one chapter keeps its Fountain whole. The older Script, Screenplay and Beat sheet fields and the beat-sheet import were retired in v117. **Scenes and beats in Narrate a story (v111):** after the beats are cut, the *Scenes* step lists the story's scenes (place, look, ambience, level) with a row to add one, and *Beats and their scenes* lists every beat with its number, Scene, Chapter and hold seconds editable in place, so beats are put into their scenes on one page; each has a row to add another. **Voices follow the Character (v110):** a take made without a voice takes its line's voice, or the voice of the Character who speaks the line; when a Character's voice changes (cast, or set by hand), the lines of that speaker that have no voice, or still have the Character's old one, take the new voice by themselves (workflow “Screenplay: a character's lines follow its voice”), and a line you gave another voice on purpose is left alone. To set every line of a Character to its voice at once, run “Screenplay: give a Character's voice to its lines” (the Casting call's *Her voice for the lines* step). **Casting call (v105):** the *Casting call* guided setup casts a story (the world and story, the roles, a look through the troupe, a shortlist of actors for a role with a note on the fit and an optional screen test, giving the role to the chosen actor with the age, costume and props, then its master picture: an AI writes the picture prompt from everything known about the role (the workflow “Screenplay: write a Character's master prompt”, saved on the Character as *Master prompt* for you to read and edit), and “Screenplay: make a Character's master” makes the picture from exactly that prompt, showing it in its approval before any image credit is spent (the reference is the actor's picture tagged for the age, else the actor's sheet), or you upload one made elsewhere; then you approve it). It is the setup for casting; Narrate a story only lists the characters. **Info cards (v103):** a beat's *Info card* fields (`info_label`, `info_title`, `info_text`, `info_icon`, `info_dim`) lay a card over the still: the picture dims, and the icon, a small gold label, the title and the sentence sit in the middle of the frame (the captions stay low, clear of them). It is drawn as one transparent picture and handed to the renderer as the beat's overlay, so it comes in after *Overlay start delay* (0.6 s if empty) and fades with *Overlay fade* (0.35 s if empty), and it **replaces** any overlay the beat, its scene or the story has for that beat (a beat with no title and no sentence has no card). *Backdrop darkness* is 0 to 1 (0.5 by default). The card is a picture overlay, so the Timeline's Overlay lane does not show it yet and a blurred backdrop is not made yet; the card shows in a draft of the beat. The worker image needs v103. **Uploaded sounds** get their length measured automatically (workflow `screenplay-measure-sound`), so the Timeline draws an uploaded effect at its real width. **Exact captions for your own takes (v100):** a take you upload or record has no word timings (only ElevenLabs gives them), so its captions were spread over the clip by length. The workflow `screenplay-time-words` now queues a `time-words` job for each such take: the transcriber worker listens to it with faster-whisper and writes when each word is spoken into the recording's metadata. The words shown are still the line's own written words (what was heard only gives their times, so a misheard name is not shown wrongly); a take with no written text gets the heard words. The transcriber needs the image of this version, with `time-words` in its `WORKER_KINDS`. **Platform presets (v99):** the story's *Picture shape* (`format`) makes the video tall (1080x1920, for Shorts, Reels and TikTok), square, 4:5 or wide (1920x1080); the pictures are cropped to fit, title and end cards and placeholder cards are made in that shape, and captions sit lower on a shape that is not a tall Short. *Loudness* (`loudness`) sets the target in LUFS (-14 is what the big platforms aim for). *Where it is going* (`platform`) makes a render say so, in plain words in its log and result (`warning`), when the film is longer than that platform takes (platforms change their limits: check the current one). A draft is half the size. **Captions (v98):** the story's *Caption style* (classic, box, highlight, none), *Caption position* (bottom keeps clear of the platforms' own buttons and text), *Caption size*, *Caption colour* and *Highlight colour* set how the burned-in captions look, and a Character's *Caption colour* shows that speaker's lines in it. The highlight style lights each word as it is spoken, from the take's word timings (an uploaded take with none spreads the caption's seconds over its words by length). Every whole-story render also keeps the captions as a subtitle (SubRip) file on the story, `subtitles`, to upload where a platform prefers the file to burned-in text. **The pause where a scene ends (v97):** the story's *Pause where a scene ends* (`scene_break_seconds`) now does what it says: after the last beat of a scene, when the next beat is in another scene, the picture is held in silence for that many seconds (nothing is added for a beat with no scene, or after the last beat). It is part of the film's length, so the render and the Timeline agree. A story that stored the old default of 1 now gets that pause; set 0 for none. **Title and end cards (v96):** a story's *Title card text* (`intro_title`, with `intro_subtitle`, `intro_seconds`) puts a title on a plain colour before the first beat, and *End card text* (`outro_title`, `outro_subtitle`, `outro_seconds`) puts a closing card, such as a call to action, after the last (*Card colour* and *Card text colour* set both). A card with no text is not made. Each is a real shot of its own length (2.5 s and 3 s by default), so the music, ambience and fades run through it and the Timeline shows it as a card, with every beat after the title starting later. A draft of just some beats shows neither. **Transitions (v94):** the story's *Transition between beats* (`transition`: cut, fade or dissolve; `transition_seconds`, 0.5 by default) says how the picture gets from one beat into the next, and a beat's own *Transition into this beat* replaces it for the change into that beat (a beat set to cut stays a cut inside a story of dissolves). A fade dips to black and back up across the edge; a dissolve melts the two pictures into each other. Neither changes how long the film is: the change is made out of the last frames of one beat and the first frames of the next, so the sound, captions and Timeline stay in step. The first beat has no transition into it, and a draft of some beats ignores the edges of what it shows. **Fades cascade (v93): the sound, then the story, then the beat, each replacing the one before.** A sound has its own `fade_in` / `fade_out`. The story can replace them: *Fade in at the start* and *Fade out at the end* (`start_fade_seconds`, `end_fade_seconds`) for the music and for whatever bed starts or ends the film, and *Ambience fade in / out* (`ambience_fade_in`, `ambience_fade_out`) for every ambience bed. A beat's *Ambience fade in / out* replace the story's for the bed under that beat. The most specific setting wins, so a fade set by the story's ambience or by a beat is not replaced by the film's start or end fade. The Timeline draws the result on each bed block. **Drafts show a plain card for a beat with no still yet (v91)**, "Beat 02 / no still yet", so a story whose pictures are not all made can still be watched for timing, sound and overlays; a normal render still needs every still and now says so. **A draft of one beat (v89):** each beat card on the Timeline has a *Draft* button (it shows when you point at the card) that renders just that beat: its picture, overlay, camera move, sound, music and captions, in seconds instead of the whole film. The same works from the render workflow with *Only these beats* (`3`, `2-4`, `1,3,5`, or `4a` for an inserted beat; `4` takes 04 and 04a). A beat draft is kept as its own video file in the library and never replaces the story's `video`, and it approves nothing. **Render a draft (v88):** the Timeline's *Render a draft* button starts the `screenplay-render` workflow for the story at quality `draft` (a fast encode of a 720x1280 copy, no full-quality file kept; it replaces the story's `video`) and shows the job's progress and an *Open the video* link when it is done: the quickest way to check an overlay, a music level or a fade as the render really does them. A beat's **Sound effects** list offers only sounds of kind `effect` (v87), and the Ambience pickers (beat, scene, story) offer only `ambience`, the Music picker only `music`: pick the kind on the sound first. The worker image needs v84 for `end_fade_seconds`.

**Voice models.** A `speech-line` has an optional `model` (an ElevenLabs model id): empty uses the connection's default (usually `eleven_multilingual_v2`); `eleven_v3` is the most expressive; `eleven_flash_v2_5`, `eleven_turbo_v2_5`, `eleven_v4_turbo` and `eleven_v3_conversational` cost half as much per character (the connection's *List models* action shows every model with its cost multiplier). The spend approval names the model. A line with delivery tags (`screenplay-take-line-tagged`) always uses `eleven_v3`. **Choosing by connection.** A workspace can hold several connections of the same provider, so extra ElevenLabs connections (Settings -> Integrations -> Connect ElevenLabs, with the key given as the reference `{{SECRET_NAME}}` to a workspace secret so no second copy is stored) can each carry a different model in their settings, for example `elevenlabs_v3` (`eleven_v3`, expressive) and `elevenlabs_flash` (`eleven_flash_v2_5`, half the cost, for drafts). A `speech-line`'s **`engine`** names the connection that records it (empty: `elevenlabs`); the line workflow runs a `speech.synthesize` capability step whose integration comes from that field, and the spend approval names the connection. A line's own `model` still wins over the connection's. Only the voice workflow chooses its connection this way: sound and music take the same model whichever connection makes them, and the image stills use `image.edit`, which a capability step may not run after an approval, so the OpenAI Images model stays that connection's setting (a change to Marvin's approvable capabilities would lift that).

**Sounds.** Only a sound whose `source` is `generated` is made by ElevenLabs. For `uploaded` or `recorded` (your own file in Take) queueing it never calls ElevenLabs: workflow `screenplay-own-sound` leaves the file exactly as it is (a browser recording stays webm) and marks the sound recorded, or says to attach a file first. Nothing in the pipeline needs mp3: ffmpeg reads webm in the mix and the render. Make an mp3 only when you want to download or share a take: run `screenplay-convert-audio` (entry, field, format) on it. Fades are applied when the sound is mixed (an audio cut or a render), not saved into the file. `screenplay-take-sound` makes an effect or ambience (ElevenLabs sound generation: the description, duration 0.5 to 30 s, `prompt_influence`, `model`, `loop`); `screenplay-take-music` composes a `music` sound (paid, needs an ElevenLabs plan that includes music; instrumental). A story plays one music bed under its whole film: put the music sound's slug in the story's `music` field; it plays at that sound's volume (0.12 if empty) and fades out over 2 seconds at the end (`fade_in` / `fade_out` on the sound change that). A sound's `fade_in` / `fade_out` also apply to an effect where it is placed and to an ambience where it starts and stops (empty: an ambience gets the short crossfade between places, an effect none). Prompts that work: concrete, one thing, with what it is on (`a single spoon tapping a wooden bowl, then quiet`); for an ambience ask for a continuous, seamless bed with no distinct events, and set `loop`. The worker image needs the version that has fades (package v36) for them to take effect.

**Voices on lines.** A new `speech-line` with a `speaker` and no `voice` takes the `voice` of the story's `cast-member` whose `speaker` is the same (workflow `screenplay-line-voice`); a voice the line already has is kept, and a character made after its lines does not reach back (set the line's voice by hand). Queueing a line then branches on its `source` (generated, uploaded or recorded, like a sound): `generated` (the default) makes a take from the line's details, which ElevenLabs then records once you approve it in the Takes setup (the line itself no longer calls ElevenLabs); `uploaded` or `recorded` is your own take, never sent to ElevenLabs. Every take, either way, is a `take` entry (`take.line` is the line's slug, `source` says where it came from): queueing an own line with audio on it makes a take record of that audio, and a take created In use with `source` uploaded or recorded sets the line's Audio and marks it recorded; the file stays as it was recorded or uploaded. So the way to add your own take is to make a `take` for the line, attach the audio, and set it In use. A new own take never takes over a line: the take in use stays until you switch it with *Use this one*, so you can record ten and choose after listening (only a line with no take in use gets its first take put in use). **A take's length** comes from the recorder when you record it in the browser, from the browser when you pick a file in the Takes setup, and from a worker otherwise: a take created with a recording and no length fires `screenplay-measure-take`, which queues the `measure-audio` job (it reads the header, or decodes a browser webm that has none) and writes `duration_seconds`; `screenplay-take-length` then copies a length that changes on the take in use to its line, so the timeline stretches to the real take. **Beat details.** A beat's *Camera move* (`move`) is the camera over its still: `from` and `to` each have `x`, `y` (0-1, where the view is centred) and `zoom` (1 = the whole frame); it has presets (Hold, Slow push in, Slow pull out, Pan left, Pan right, Drift up) and empty means a slow push in. A beat's *Ambience volume* overrides the scene's level for that beat (0 mutes it). The story's *Music* and the scene's *Ambience* are pick lists of sounds of that kind with a play button to hear the one chosen. The pages of a story, chapter, scene, beat and speech line list what points at them (a story's chapters, beats, scenes, speech lines, takes, characters, sounds and revisions; a beat's lines) from the type's `children` (v128 added a story's characters, sounds and revisions): the link itself lives in the child's `story`, `chapter`, `scene`, `beat` or `line` field. A take is one attempt and carries its own details: its Words (starting as the line's, change them to improvise), Direction, voice, model and voice settings; the line stays the script. A take with source generated and status queued (made from the Takes setup's *Make it with ElevenLabs*, or set back to queued to redo) is recorded by ElevenLabs from those fields (workflows `screenplay-make-take` and `screenplay-remake-take`): you approve the spend and then Keep or Redo, and Keep makes it the take in use. The spend is a Make it / Cancel choice: cancel it, or let ElevenLabs fail, and the take becomes a `draft` (not made) instead of staying queued; set it to queued to try again. Any number of takes can belong to one line. Each take card has **Use this one** (a recorded take becomes the one the line plays) and **Try again** (a draft is queued again). In a line's voice list the speaker's own voice comes first, then the story's cast. In the story timeline, clicking a line opens its Takes directly (the Lines lane's `open`, v75). A story has a **Cover image** (role `cover`): it is the story's card in the guided setups, and a beat's still is its card in the Beat step. In a long story the Takes setup narrows the choice first: Story, then an optional Chapter, then an optional Beat, then the Line (or click the line in the timeline and skip all that). Each take in the Takes setup has a *Remove* button that moves it to the Trash (restorable); if it was the take in use, the line keeps its audio until you choose another. The Takes setup shows the line above its takes (text, direction, delivery text, voice, model), lists them with the one In use marked, names a new take after its line ("01 Granny: … · take 2"), and keeps a take's length (a recording brings its recorder's count, an uploaded file is measured in the browser). A take's and a line's voice lists put the voices cast in the story first (`ref_via`), and a take made without a voice starts with its line's (`screenplay-take-voice`). A speech line's page has a **Takes** button (the guided setup `takes-of-a-line`, v71): hear the line's takes, record or upload your own (it becomes the line's take only when the line has none: `screenplay-use-new-take`; otherwise the take in use stays until you press *Use this one*), or make one with ElevenLabs. The chain is Take, Speech line, Beat, Story (an episode is a story in a series; its chapters sit inside it), World. That is how to get a free guide take before paying for the real voice: set `source` back to `generated` and queue the line when you want ElevenLabs. A voice with no `elevenlabs_voice_id` is never sent to ElevenLabs either. The batch recorder `screenplay-record-lines` skips lines whose source is uploaded or recorded.

**Is a story ready to make? (v128).** Run the workflow *Screenplay: check a story is ready* on a story before making masters, stills or recordings. It reads the records only (no AI, no spend) and writes the story's *Ready to make?* note: what **stops** a picture or recording (no World, Setting or Premise; a Character on screen with no actor; a speaker with no voice, or a voice that is not in the workspace or has no ElevenLabs id; a cast that names someone who is not a Character; a sound to generate with no prompt) and what is **worth a look** (no costume or description, no master yet, beats with nobody in the cast or no expression or focus, scenes with no set dressing, sounds with no length, the same sound described twice). Run it again after fixing. It cannot see what the ElevenLabs plan allows; that still shows only when a recording is refused.

**Voices follow the actor (v131).** A Character made from an actor, or given one while it has no voice, takes the actor's voice and its sample. When an actor's voice later changes, *an actor's new voice reaches the characters who play them* (`screenplay-actor-voice-reaches-characters`) gives it, and its sample, to every Character cast from that actor that has no voice or still has the actor's old one, and the lines of that speaker follow (`screenplay-lines-follow-voice`). A Character given another voice on purpose is left alone; *Sync line voices* is the way to force every line. A voice-only Character (a narrator) has no actor, so its voice is set by hand.

**Batch approvals (v132).** Making a film used to ask twice for every picture and sound (spend, then keep). Now: *Screenplay: make the stills of a story* (`screenplay-make-stills`) lists the next 30 beats that are waiting (status draft, with their picture described) in one approval and queues them all; *Screenplay: make the sounds of a story* (`screenplay-make-sounds`) does the same for sounds that are waiting (draft, a prompt, not an upload, not music). Each beat or sound it queues carries `spend_approved: yes`, so its own workflow (`screenplay-take-still`, `screenplay-take-sound`) skips its spend approval and its keep approval and leaves the result at `made` (a still) or `recorded` (a sound); the flag becomes `used` the moment the making starts, so a redo or a beat you queue by hand later asks again as before. Look at the results (the story's Timeline shows each beat's picture; a sound's page plays it), set any you want redone to `redo`, then *Screenplay: keep the stills that were made* / *keep the sounds that were made* (`screenplay-keep-stills`, `screenplay-keep-sounds`) approves every one still at `made` / `recorded` in one step ("Keep all"). A story with no World, or with nothing waiting, is told so and nothing is spent. **Review on the Timeline (v133):** the story's Timeline view has `spine.review`, so a beat whose status is `made` (a batch-made still waiting to be looked at) shows **Keep** and **Redo** on its card: Keep sets the beat to `approved`, Redo sets it to `redo` (the Redo workflow then makes it again in two or three minutes and asks to approve that spend, since a batch's approval is used up). A kept or redone card loses its buttons at once. Voices already batch: *Record the lines*. The per-item way (queue a beat, approve its spend and keep) still works.

**People, beat details and continuity (v134).** (1) A Character has **Age**, **Sex** and **Age state** as fields: the import fills them from the screenplay when it introduces the person (`CHET (20s), a young cabin boy` gives 20s, male, young; the sex only when a pronoun or a word like boy or woman says it, the age state from the age: young under 30, mid under 55, old after), and a Character made before these fields existed gets them on the next import, only where they are empty. (2) *Screenplay: write the expression and focus of a story's beats* (`screenplay-write-beat-details`, job `write-beat-details`) asks the workspace AI (the plain `chat` agent, ten beats to a question) to propose each beat's **expression** (what the people are doing and how they look) and **focus** (the one object the picture is about) from its words, the cast's looks, the lines spoken and the place; one approval, the next 40 beats at a time, only empty fields are filled, never what you wrote, and `dry_run` on the job shows the proposals without writing. Read them and edit what is off before making stills; it uses no image or voice credit. (3) A beat has **Changes from here on** (`changes`): write what stays changed (`Valerie has lost her hat`) and the still workflow tells the model, for this beat and every later one, so a lost hat stays lost without each beat saying "hatless".

**Voice plan and picture size (v136).** *Screenplay: check a story is ready, with the ElevenLabs plan* (`screenplay-check-ready-plan`) first makes ElevenLabs' free usage lookup (tier, characters left this period, when it resets; nothing is recorded) and then runs the readiness check with it: it says whether the lines still to record (their text, counted in characters) fit in what is left, and, on a free plan, names any library (professional) voice in use, which ElevenLabs refuses only when a recording is asked for. It needs the ElevenLabs connection; the plain readiness check does not. *Make an actor's sheet* takes an optional **size** (`1536x1024` for a landscape sheet; empty keeps the connection's size): an integration step may set its own picture size since OpenAI Images 0.6.0, and the approval names it.

# What was added after the stages: v128 onward

The stages, the records and the first workflows are in marvin.md. This file holds what came after, one paragraph per release, newest last: the readiness check, batch approvals, voices following the actor, Fountain checks, the story's ambience bed, reading while recording, recording one character's lines, tidier forms.

**Is a story ready to make? (v128).** Run the workflow *Screenplay: check a story is ready* on a story before making masters, stills or recordings. It reads the records only (no AI, no spend) and writes the story's *Ready to make?* note: what **stops** a picture or recording (no World, Setting or Premise; a Character on screen with no actor; a speaker with no voice, or a voice that is not in the workspace or has no ElevenLabs id; a cast that names someone who is not a Character; a sound to generate with no prompt) and what is **worth a look** (no costume or description, no master yet, beats with nobody in the cast or no expression or focus, scenes with no set dressing, sounds with no length, the same sound described twice). Run it again after fixing. It cannot see what the ElevenLabs plan allows; that still shows only when a recording is refused.

**Voices follow the actor (v131).** A Character made from an actor, or given one while it has no voice, takes the actor's voice and its sample. When an actor's voice later changes, *an actor's new voice reaches the characters who play them* (`screenplay-actor-voice-reaches-characters`) gives it, and its sample, to every Character cast from that actor that has no voice or still has the actor's old one, and the lines of that speaker follow (`screenplay-lines-follow-voice`). A Character given another voice on purpose is left alone; *Sync line voices* is the way to force every line. A voice-only Character (a narrator) has no actor, so its voice is set by hand.

**Batch approvals (v132).** Making a film used to ask twice for every picture and sound (spend, then keep). Now: *Screenplay: make the stills of a story* (`screenplay-make-stills`) lists the next 30 beats that are waiting (status draft, with their picture described) in one approval and queues them all; *Screenplay: make the sounds of a story* (`screenplay-make-sounds`) does the same for sounds that are waiting (draft, a prompt, not an upload, not music). Each beat or sound it queues carries `spend_approved: yes`, so its own workflow (`screenplay-take-still`, `screenplay-take-sound`) skips its spend approval and its keep approval and leaves the result at `made` (a still) or `recorded` (a sound); the flag becomes `used` the moment the making starts, so a redo or a beat you queue by hand later asks again as before. Look at the results (the story's Timeline shows each beat's picture; a sound's page plays it), set any you want redone to `redo`, then *Screenplay: keep the stills that were made* / *keep the sounds that were made* (`screenplay-keep-stills`, `screenplay-keep-sounds`) approves every one still at `made` / `recorded` in one step ("Keep all"). A story with no World, or with nothing waiting, is told so and nothing is spent. **Review on the Timeline (v133):** the story's Timeline view has `spine.review`, so a beat whose status is `made` (a batch-made still waiting to be looked at) shows **Keep** and **Redo** on its card: Keep sets the beat to `approved`, Redo sets it to `redo` (the Redo workflow then makes it again in two or three minutes and asks to approve that spend, since a batch's approval is used up). A kept or redone card loses its buttons at once. Voices already batch: *Record the lines*. The per-item way (queue a beat, approve its spend and keep) still works.

**People, beat details and continuity (v134).** (1) A Character has **Age**, **Sex** and **Age state** as fields: the import fills them from the screenplay when it introduces the person (`CHET (20s), a young cabin boy` gives 20s, male, young; the sex only when a pronoun or a word like boy or woman says it, the age state from the age: young under 30, mid under 55, old after), and a Character made before these fields existed gets them on the next import, only where they are empty. (2) *Screenplay: write the expression and focus of a story's beats* (`screenplay-write-beat-details`, job `write-beat-details`) asks the workspace AI (the plain `chat` agent, ten beats to a question) to propose each beat's **expression** (what the people are doing and how they look) and **focus** (the one object the picture is about) from its words, the cast's looks, the lines spoken and the place; one approval, the next 40 beats at a time, only empty fields are filled, never what you wrote, and `dry_run` on the job shows the proposals without writing. Read them and edit what is off before making stills; it uses no image or voice credit. (3) A beat has **Changes from here on** (`changes`): write what stays changed (`Valerie has lost her hat`) and the still workflow tells the model, for this beat and every later one, so a lost hat stays lost without each beat saying "hatless".

**Voice plan and picture size (v136).** *Screenplay: check a story is ready, with the ElevenLabs plan* (`screenplay-check-ready-plan`) first makes ElevenLabs' free usage lookup (tier, characters left this period, when it resets; nothing is recorded) and then runs the readiness check with it: it says whether the lines still to record (their text, counted in characters) fit in what is left, and, on a free plan, names any library (professional) voice in use, which ElevenLabs refuses only when a recording is asked for. It needs the ElevenLabs connection; the plain readiness check does not. *Make an actor's sheet* takes an optional **size** (`1536x1024` for a landscape sheet; empty keeps the connection's size): an integration step may set its own picture size since OpenAI Images 0.6.0, and the approval names it.

**Fountain checks (v138).** Every import, import preview and revision check now reads the Fountain for two things it would otherwise read differently from what was meant, and names each under **Check:** with its line number and the fix: a line in capitals that stands alone or ends in a full stop (`CRUNCH.`) or sits above text, which becomes a *speaker* (a recording is then made of the words after it): start it with `!`; and a line that is a place and a time in capitals without `INT.` or `EXT.` (`UNDERWATER - CONTINUOUS`), which is not a scene: write `EXT. UNDERWATER - CONTINUOUS`, or start it with a dot. It reads the text only and changes nothing; legitimate cues (`NARRATOR (V.O.)`, `MRS. HUDSON`), transitions, `SUPER:` lines, `THE END` and the title page are left alone, and at most twelve are listed.

**Reading while you record (v139).** In the Takes setup, *Record…* opens the recorder with the take's **Words** and **Direction** shown large above its controls, as they stand in the form when you press it (so a change you made for this take is what you read). The words are the biggest text and the direction below them in italics; **A−** and **A+** change the size and the choice is remembered; a long text scrolls inside its own panel so the Record button stays in reach. The setup asks for this with `add.file.script` (up to three fields of the add form); a recording box without it opens the recorder as before. It needs Marvin with the reading panel (marvin#186).

**The story's ambience through the scenes, and ducking (v140).** The story's own ambience (the waves under the whole film) is one bed that never stops. A **Scene** can set **The story's ambience in this scene: level (0–1)** and **heard from inside (muffled)**, and a **Beat** its own **level** (it wins over its scene's; 0 is silence for that beat): the bed eases to the new level over a second at the scene's edge and back, so there is no fade out and in per scene, and a muffled scene crossfades the *same* sound between as-it-is and low-passed (700 Hz), so it never restarts. Example: waves at 0.2 for the story, 0.1 and muffled in the captain's cabin, 0.03 and muffled in the hold. These are **not** the scene's own *Ambience* (a separate bed, layered on top, with its own level and muffling): they change only the story's bed. Resolution for the level: beat, else scene, else the story's. The Story's **Ambience under speech** (`ambience_ducking`: none, light, strong) lowers every ambience bed (the story's and the scenes') under each spoken line by about 9 or 18 dB, the same dip and timing as *Music under speech*; lines closer than 0.7 s count as one. The render does all of this (`story_bed_in` in the puller; `story_bed_level`, `story_bed_muffle` and the duck in `build.py`); the Timeline's ambience lane draws the story's bed as steps (v141), each tagged with who set it (the story, a scene or a beat) and hatched where it is heard from inside, with a tooltip like "set by the scene The hold"; Play follows the levels. The two new level fields have a drag slider beside the number box (empty is still "not set"; × clears it).

**Recording one character's lines (v142).** In the Takes setup, after the story there is an optional **Character** step: choose one (the narrator, say) and the line list shows just that character's lines across the whole story, in order, each with its status (`draft` or `recorded`) under it, so you can sit down and record all of them one after another. Skip the step to choose by chapter and beat as before; a chapter or beat chosen as well narrows the list further. It works because each speech line now names its Character in `character` (the cast member's slug): the import sets it on new lines, and an import run again fills it in on lines made earlier (only where empty, in one change together with anything else the line was missing). A line written by hand has none and is simply not in a character's list.

**Tidier forms (v143).** The records that carry sound now fold it away the same way the Story's *Sound and language* section does: a **Scene** and a **Beat** have a **Sound** section (ambience, its levels and fades, the story's-ambience level and muffling, the beat's sound effects and music level) and an **Overlay** section (the same name the Story uses); a **Sound** has **Playback** (volume, loop, fades) and **Making it** (model, prompt influence); a **Speech line**, a **Take**, a **Character** and an **Actor** have a **Voice** section (voice, model, stability and so on). A section is folded until you open it, says how many of its fields hold something, and opens by itself when one of them has an error; what is said, what is in the picture and the other main fields stay in the open. Every level field (0 to 1: the ambience, music and sound levels, overlay strength, the voice's stability, similarity and style) and the voice speed has a **drag slider** beside its number box: drag or type; an empty box still means *not set* (the slider is dimmed, × clears it). The Story no longer has the *Screenplay* and *Beat sheet (draft)* fields: nothing read them (the Fountain field and the import replaced them), and every story had them empty.

**Sounds for a story (v144).** The guided setup *Sounds for a story* (also the **Sounds** button on a story) starts from what the story needs: the sounds the import found in its words (effects, a bed under each place, a score), shown as cards you can play, with status and where each came from. Add what the import missed (your own file, one to make later, or one to make now), set the bed under each scene (its ambience, level and muffle), set the story-wide ambience and music (fades, ducking), then make everything waiting with one approval and keep what you like with another. The last step checks nothing is waiting, kept or marked to redo. A sound is at most 30 seconds (the ElevenLabs limit); an ambience or music bed with *Loop smoothly* on is repeated to fill the scene or story in a render, so make it a steady 20 to 30 seconds. The setup ends on the Timeline, where effects are placed at a time with their own level.

**How loud a sound's take is (v145).** A **Sound** shows two meters right under its **Take**: the take's **average level** and its **loudest moment**, in dB below full scale (0 is the loudest a file can be; the bar turns amber near the top and red at it), and the **Volume** slider beside them, no longer folded away under Playback: listen, watch the meters, drag the volume. They are measured by the worker (workflow *Measure how loud a sound's take is*, job kind `measure-level`) whenever a take is attached: made, uploaded, recorded, or a new one after a redo. A sound whose take was attached before v145 is measured once when it is next given a take. The meters are read-only; the Volume is what the mix uses.

**Sliders on the fades (v146).** The ambience *fade in* and *fade out* (on the story and on a beat) and a sound's *fade in* and *fade out* have a drag slider beside their box, from 0 to 10 seconds in steps of a tenth. A fade is a few seconds at most, and ten seconds is already a long one, so the slider stops there.

**The Takes setup shows the whole line (v147).** The Line step's list used to show only each line's title, which is cut at about 50 characters (a line's speaker and its first words, sometimes mid-word). Each line now has its whole text under the title, then whether it has a take yet. It needs Marvin's change that lets a step's `subtitle` name up to three fields and draws it in a plain list.

**Choosing a character skips the chapter and beat steps (v148).** In the Takes setup, choosing a Character now goes straight to the Line step, which lists all of that character's lines in the story in order, each with its status. The Chapter and Beat steps are for finding a line when no character was chosen, and are skipped when one was (they offered every chapter and beat, including ones the character says nothing in).

**A new line is linked to its character (v149).** The Takes setup finds a character's lines by the line's *Character* field. Lines imported before v142 had none, and a line added by hand has none either: workflow *Link a new line to its character* fills it when a line is made with a speaker (the character of the story whose speaker is the same; one already set is kept). The lines that existed were filled once, from their speaker.

**Inside or outside, and a script that reads back as itself (v150).** A **Scene** has an *Inside or outside* field (interior, exterior, both). The import fills it from the heading (`INT.` → interior, `EXT.`/`EST.` → exterior, `INT./EXT.` → both) instead of writing "(interior)" into the Place, and an interior scene's own ambience and the story's are muffled by default (untick them on the scene). The first picture of a scene now says it in words, `Interior: Captain's Cabin, night. A lantern hangs…`, not `INT. CAPTAIN'S CABIN - NIGHT.`: an image model reads words, and the beat's title (`Beat 1003: A lantern hangs…`) no longer starts with the heading. The other way: the exported script writes a scene that says inside or outside as `INT. CAPTAIN'S CABIN #2#` (one that does not keeps the forced `.CAPTAIN'S CABIN #2#`). The *complete* script (every detail, as Fountain) now reads back through the import as the same chapters, beats, lines, characters, sounds, holds and transitions: it no longer says a scene's heading twice, no longer keeps "Delivery:" in the action, and writes each transition (`CUT TO:`) where it was. Not kept yet: the time of day in a heading (`- NIGHT`) has no field of its own. The default (production) script is the one to share and does not read back as the same beats (it has no chapter lines and no picture breaks).

**Scenes with no ambience are exported as scenes (v151).** The script writer picked a story's scenes by whether they had an ambience, so a scene with none was missed and its beats were written under a bare forced heading named from its slug (`.THE DEVILS SHOALS BENEATH THE WRECK`). It now picks a scene by the fields only a scene has, and writes every heading from the scene: `EXT. BENEATH THE WRECK #6#`.

**Time of day on a beat (v152).** A **Beat** has a *Time of day* field (Night, Dawn, Continuous, Later…): the import fills it from the heading the beat is under (`INT. CABIN - NIGHT` → Night), and the script writes it back (`INT. CAPTAIN'S CABIN - NIGHT #2#`). The same place at another time of day is a new heading in the script, as it is in the Fountain. Together with *Inside or outside* on the scene, the complete script now reads back with the same headings it was made from. A beat made before this has none until it is filled in (or the story is imported again); a beat with none writes a heading with no time.

**What reads back, and what does not (v153).** The *complete* script is tested against the most detailed screenplay the import understands (`tests/test_script_roundtrip.py`): it is imported, exported from the records it made, and imported again, and the title page, chapters and their synopses, headings (inside or outside, time of day), pictures, holds, transitions, every line with its parentheticals and extensions (`NARRATOR (V.O.)`), sounds with their times, sung lyrics (`~`), characters and places come back the same. A transition is written above a chapter's `#` heading, never below it (a section followed by a transition is read as the writer's note about the scene before). **Written out but not read back in** (notes, `[[ ]]`, for a person and for the records they were made from): a character's costume, props, rules and actor; a scene's place, light, set dressing, continuity and ambience levels; a beat's focus, expression, camera and cast; a line's voice. They are in the file so nothing in the records is lost from it, but a new import does not fill them from it. A `## SCENE 1:` label and a synopsis under a scene heading (only the first synopsis of a chapter is kept, as its summary) are not kept, and neither is a closing `FADE OUT.`.

**Check the Fountain (v154).** A Fountain is checked before it is read: `python -m storyrender check file.fountain` (no Marvin needed), the setup step *Check the Fountain* (the report goes into the story's **Fountain check**), or the workflows *Screenplay: check the Fountain* and *check some Fountain text* (job kind `check-fountain`, for the Marvin CLI, the web app or an AI over MCP; its `result.report` and `result.findings` hold what it found). It finds a speaker in lower case, a note or bracket left open, a scene number used for two places, a transition with no colon, a Hold or Sound time that is not a number, a section with no title, a heading with no place, invisible characters, a speaker spelled two ways and a missing Setting or Premise, each with its line and what to write. The import now stops on the serious ones and lists them (`accept_errors: yes` goes on anyway); a preview and a revision check only report. Written in `references/fountain.md`, section 12.

**Waveforms on the Timeline (v155).** A take shows its waveform behind its label in the Timeline's **Lines** and **Sounds** lanes: the loudest and quietest point of each of 240 stretches, mirrored round the middle. The worker works it out once, when a take is attached (workflow *Draw a take as a waveform*, job kind `make-waveform`, ffmpeg), and keeps it on the entry in the **Waveform** field (a Sound, a Speech line and a Take have one, under *Made by Marvin*; the audiowaveform JSON format, about 3 KB). The browser cannot decode a stored take itself, which is why it is kept as data. A take attached before v155 has none until it is given a new take or its Waveform is made (queue a `make-waveform` job with `args.entry`). Needs Marvin's Timeline waveform (a lane's `waveform` key).

**A speech line's volume and muffling, and a take's link to its line (v156).** A **Speech line** has **Volume** (0 to 1, a slider; empty is as recorded) and **Muffled** (the highs cut, as if heard through a wall) in its **Sound** section. They are settings of the line, so they apply to every take of it and to a take used later: the render mixes the line at that level and muffled, and the Timeline's Lines lane plays it at the level (Play cannot muffle; a render does). A **Take**'s *Line* field is now a pick list of its story's lines with an **Open ↗** link to the line, so from a take you go to the line, where the settings every take shares live. A level for one quiet recording on its own (a take's trim, multiplied with the line's) is not there yet.

**A speech line's Beat is a pick list (v157).** A **Speech line**'s *Beat* field lists the story's beats with an **Open ↗** link, instead of a slug to type. To move a line to another beat, open the line and choose the beat: its takes go with it, the Timeline and the production and complete script exports follow. The *written* export replays the Fountain as it was imported, so it keeps the line where it was written until the Fountain is edited.

**Music has levels and fades like ambience (v158).** The music is set the way the ambience is. **Story:** *Music level* (0–1; empty is the music sound's own) and *Music fade in / out* (seconds, for the music only). **Scene:** *Music level* for the scene. **Beat:** *Music level* (it had that) and now *Music fade in / out* (how long the music takes to ease to this beat's level and away from it; it only matters where the level changes). The level of a beat is its own, else its scene's, else the story's, else the sound's; 0 is silence. A fade is the beat's, else the story's music fade, else the film's start and end fade, else the sound's. There is no *muffled* for music: a score is not heard through a wall. The Timeline's Music lane plays the story's level and fades (a scene's level is not drawn there yet). The audio-only export now follows the story's *Loudness* like the video does (it always used -16 before).

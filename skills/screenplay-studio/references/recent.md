# What was added after the stages: v180 onward

One paragraph per release, newest last. v128 to v179 (the readiness check, batch approvals, voices following the actor, Fountain checks, the story's ambience bed, reading while recording, recording one character's lines, tidier forms, plain cards, transitions and more) are in recent-older.md.

**Files beside the text (v180; needs Marvin #246).** A form step now draws a file field with the entry's own asset control (choose, upload, record), so three uploads are no longer steps of their own. *Narrate a story*: the Fountain file is under the Fountain text on *Story details* (the step *Or upload a Fountain file* is gone; to replace a file, choose or upload another there). *Open call*: the actor's picture and the sheet are one step, *Actor sheet and picture*. *Casting call*: the master picture is three steps, not five: *Write the master prompt (optional)*, *The master picture* (choose or upload a picture, or read and edit the prompt), *Make the master picture from the prompt (optional)*; *Approve the master* shows the picture again.
**Going round again (v180; needs Marvin #247).** The last step of every setup has a button that starts it over from its pick: *Work on another actor*, *Cast another role*, *Takes of another line*, *Narrate another story*, *Sounds for another story*, *Lines of another beat*. The choices before that pick stay (the world, the story), the rest are forgotten.

**An empty step says where to go, and a paid button says so (v181; needs Marvin #251 and #252).** *Takes of a line*: a story with no characters, chapters or beats yet says so and links to *Narrate a story* (they are made when the Fountain is brought in); a beat with no lines says so and links to *Lines of a beat*. *Lines of a beat*: an empty beat says it may be a picture with no words, and links to *Narrate a story*. *Casting call*: an empty troupe or shortlist links to *Open call* (a new actor is made there); *Give the role to the actor* now shows the role's **Voice** (empty takes the actor's; choose one for a role with no actor, such as a narrator; empty it after changing the actor), so *Voice for the lines* links back to it when the role has none. The buttons that send to a paid outside service are marked, show what goes and warn when it is missing: *Make it with ElevenLabs* and *Try again* on a take (the Words and the Voice: a take's Direction is a note for people and is never sent), *Make this one*, *Make it again* and *Make it now with ElevenLabs* on a sound (its description and length), *Write the prompt with AI* (the role's costume, props and rules, with the actor's sheet, the story and the world's style) and *Make the master* (the master prompt, to OpenAI Images). The ElevenLabs and image workflows still ask for their spend approval before anything is spent; writing the prompt with AI runs as soon as its button is pressed.

**An actor's sheet with AI, for people and animals (v182).** *Open call* has a step after *Actor sheet and picture*: **Make the sheet with AI (optional)**, which runs *Screenplay: make an actor's sheet* on the actor (marked as paid: it shows the Base, Age, Build, Look and Distinguishing features that go, and needs Build filled in). The workflow draws on the world's base sheet for the actor's Base, the asset whose slug is `<world>-base-<base>` (`storyworld-base-woman`, `storyworld-base-dog`). A **person** is drawn as before (four views in a 2 by 2 grid, from base, age, build, hair, skin tone, look, distinguishing features and baseline wardrobe, with `style_actor`, default `arthur`, for the house style). An **animal**, an actor whose Base is one of the input `animal_bases` (default `dog,cat,horse,bird`; lower case, as the base is named), is drawn as three full-body views in a row (front, right-side profile, back) in a neutral standing quadruped pose on cream, with no collar, clothes, props or text, from base, age, build, look (the coat) and distinguishing features (the markings); hair, skin tone and wardrobe are not sent, no toy brand is named, and the picture is landscape (1536x1024) unless `size` says otherwise. An animal takes no other actor's sheet unless `style_animal` names one. When Build is empty or the base sheet is not in the Assets library, one card says which, names the slug it looked for and spends nothing: make the base sheet (in Storyworld from `cast/bases/<base>-base.prompt.txt`), upload it, set its slug and run again. Otherwise the spend card shows the exact prompt first, then Keep (approves the sheet) or Redo (leaves it a draft: run it again). The workflow stays at ten steps, Marvin's default limit per workflow.

**Make it now makes it (v182).** In *Sounds for a story*, *Make it now with ElevenLabs* saves a new sound already queued, but making a sound listened only for an edit (`entry_updated`), and a new entry sends `entry_created`, so the sound sat at queued and nothing was made. Two twins now hear the new one: `screenplay-take-sound-created` and `screenplay-take-music-created`, the same conditions and steps as `screenplay-take-sound` and `screenplay-take-music` on `entry_created`. A sound queued by an edit (*Make this one*, the batch) is still made by the original; one sound never starts both. Takes already had this pair (`screenplay-make-take` on create, `screenplay-remake-take` on edit); no button makes a speech line already queued, so `screenplay-take-line` needs no twin.

**Who is cast, lines that read in order, and a "no" that lets go (v183, needs Marvin's highlight by reference, add-form order, `only_with` and six `approve` workflows).** (1) *Casting call*, *Look through the troupe*: an actor already cast in this story carries a mark with the role (*Cast as Granny*; *Cast as Granny, Old Granny* for one who plays it at two ages), from the story's Characters whose Actor is that actor. (2) *Lines of a beat*: the add form reads Speaker, Words, Direction, Order, with Direction right under the words instead of folded away in *How it is said* (the form follows the order the step lists). (3) The Beat pick offers only the beats that have lines; a beat that is only a picture gets its first line from the **Lines** button on the beat itself. (4) *Sounds for a story* shows the spend question of a sound made with *Make it now* on the page (it is asked by the `-created` twins, which the step did not list), so there is nothing to look for under Automation. (5) Saying **Deny** to a spend question (or leaving it until it expires) puts the sound, music cue or take back to `draft` at once, so it no longer sits at `queued` and its button can start it again. The run goes on after the answer, so each of those paying workflows (`screenplay-take-sound`, `screenplay-take-music`, both `-created` twins, `screenplay-make-take`, `screenplay-remake-take`) has a step right after its spend question that does it; the Keep / Redo question and an approval are untouched. A still is not reset this way: `screenplay-take-still` already has more than Marvin's ten steps, and one more would push its Keep question past a workspace's raised limit, so set a beat whose still was denied back to draft by hand.

**Text that matches the import (v183).** Text-only follow-ups to Studio #162 (one character per name, no sounds from capitals, readable speaker handles, one-line job results): the references no longer say a loud word in capitals makes a sound (only a `[[Sound:]]` note or `the sound of X` does, and a capitals line standing alone is read as action), and say that two speakers who would share a one-word handle keep all their words joined by hyphens (`DR HALE` is Dr-hale), while a story brought in before v183 keeps the handles it has.

**Speaker names and transitions (v183).** A possessive speaker keeps one word (`A GIRL'S VOICE` -> a-girls-voice); a story brought in earlier keeps the handles it has. Any line in capitals ending in `TO:` is a transition: a dissolve or a fade when it says so, otherwise a cut with its words kept, with nothing to check. `INTERCUT` lines stay action in the picture.

**Shot direction on a beat (v183).** A beat has **Shot direction** (`direction`, in *The shot*): the shot words a screenplay writes in capitals on a line of their own (ON WILL AND SANDRA, UNDER THE TABLE, VARIOUS SHOTS), as written. The still's prompt reads it right after THIS MOMENT (`Shot direction (where the camera looks): …`, `none` when empty). A workspace whose Beat type is older gets the field from the `beat-direction-field` blueprint on update (fields a beat already has are left alone). The import filling it in comes in a later release; until then it is written by hand.

**Paid jobs show on their page from the click to the result, and every Redo too (v184, needs Marvin #262 and #263).** *Sounds for a story* and *Takes of a line* name what a card being made says (`working`: `queued` → *Being made: anything it asks appears at the top of this step*, `redo` → *Making it again: the new one starts within two minutes…*). The page watches those cards and draws itself again when one moves on: the spend question appears at the top of the step without a refresh, and once it is answered there the page follows that run (*Running…*, then the Keep / Redo question with the sound to play, or *Failed* with the reason). A sound whose run fails goes back to draft (an `on_failure` step on the four sound workflows, as the take workflows already had), so *Make this one* can start it again. **Make it again** on a recorded sound now sets it queued and starts at once (it used to go through `redo` and wait two minutes). A Redo answered on the Keep question still waits about two minutes for the requeue, and the card says so; Marvin now lets a Redo be answered any number of times (the second Redo of the same sound used to do nothing). In *Open call* (Make the sheet) and *Casting call* (Make the master) a **Redo** on the Keep question starts the step's run again at once (`redo: "Redo"`): it asks before spending again, then shows the new picture.

**The Fountain check says how long the import takes (v184).** The Fountain check also says about how long bringing the story in will take. `EXT/INT.`, `I/E.` and `E/I.` read as inside-and-outside headings, like `INT./EXT.`, and a non-breaking or zero-width space reads as the space it looks like.

**An import that lost the connection carries on (v184, with MarvinWorker #15).** If bringing the chapters in stops with "lost the connection to Marvin after N records", run it again (*Bring these chapters in*): it makes only what is missing, under the same numbers, and its log starts "Carrying on: N records already there". Nothing is made twice.

**Change the prompt before it is spent, and Redo with changes (v185, with Marvin #265).** Every spend approval that makes one thing (a sound, music, a take, a still, a master, an actor's sheet, a cover) now shows the exact prompt in a box above Approve, on the Approvals page and on the guided setup's card. Leave it and the call is what it always was, word for word; change it and the call gets your text. The Keep / Redo question shows the prompt that was used: change it and press **Redo with changes**, and the next try starts from your text (once; the next run after that is built from the records again). A change made there is not written back to the sound's description, the beat or the Master prompt: to keep it for good, change the record. A sound, picture or take made after an approval keeps the whole prompt it was made from, who approved it and whether the prompt was changed (the asset page says so). A page that already had the spend approved (*Make it now*, a batch) goes on without asking, as before. The batch approvals (*Make the stills*, *Make the sounds*, *Record lines*) still ask once for the lot, without boxes: each item's own run uses its own prompt.

**Make an ambience or music where the beds are chosen (v186, needs Marvin #264).** *Sounds for a story* has two more steps: **Ambience to choose from**, right before *The bed under each place*, and **Music to choose from**, right before *Ambience and music for the whole story*. Each shows only the story's sounds of that kind (a gallery's `where`) and opens with a short form to make one: describe it (an ambience also takes a length; ElevenLabs picks the music's), then *Save my own sound*, *Add to the list, make it later* or *Make it now with ElevenLabs* (it asks before anything is spent, on the same page, then asks you to keep it). The kind is set for you and the sound is made to loop. What you make there is in the pick list of the next step at once, before it is even made. *What the story calls for* still lists every sound.

**Everything made from a script in the script's order (v186, needs Marvin #264).** Every pick, list and gallery of the guided setups that shows what an import made now follows the script, never the alphabet or numbers read as text: chapters and beats by their number (1001 before 10001), a beat's lines by their order (lines of several beats beat by beat, then in the order spoken; never by title or speaker), and scenes, sounds and characters in the order the import made them (a step's `sort: {by}`, asked of Marvin's entries query): scenes as their places first appear, a beat's sounds in beat order, then the score, then the beds; characters as the import makes them, which today is by their speaker handle, not by first appearance. Where: *Narrate a story* (Scenes, Beats and their scenes, Sounds), *Sounds for a story* (all four lists), *Casting call* (the characters and the pick of one), *Takes of a line* (character, chapter, beat and line picks) and *Lines in a beat* (the beat pick and its lines). The workflows that list a story's lines for their voices and its sounds to make take them in that order too. Something added by hand later (a scene the Fountain does not name) comes after what the import made. *Narrate a story*: **Clear what the import made** is now the last step, after *Check what you have*, and its help starts "Only if you want to start over".

**From one guided setup to another, and back (v191, needs Marvin #276).** A step can link to a step of another setup, taking along what is chosen (`see_also`), and that setup then says at its top *You came from …, step N. ← Back to it* until you go back or leave it. *Narrate a story*, **Sounds: are these read right?** → *Sounds for a story* (What the story calls for), with the story: make or upload music, ambience and effects there, then Back. *Sounds for a story*, **Story** → *Narrate a story* (Story details) with the story and its world and series: **Bring a script in**. *Narrate a story*, **Check what you have** → *Casting call* (The roles) with the world and story. *Casting call*, **Look through the troupe** → *Open call* (The troupe) with the world, to add an actor. *Lines of a beat*, **Lines** → *Takes of a line* (Line) with the story and beat; *Takes of a line*, **Takes** → *Lines of a beat* with the story and the line's beat. **Which chapters:** *Preview the chapters* and *Make the chapters, beats and lines* show a card with the story's *Chapters to bring in* (or *All the chapters* when it is empty), read when the button is pressed; the field is also on **Story details** now, so it can be set before the preview.

**The script in the box or as a file, one or the other (v193, needs Marvin #275).** A story's *Fountain* and *Fountain file* take turns (`exclusive_with`): while one holds the script, *Story details* and the story's own page show the other closed, with a line saying where the script is and how to free the field ("“Fountain file” holds it. Remove the file to write it here instead."); emptying one opens the other at once. A save that would fill both is refused, naming the two fields, from the form, the API, a workflow or the AI; a story that already held both shows both with a warning and still saves. The import's *Fountain text and an uploaded Fountain file* stop stays for such a story. An uploaded script is now readable in Marvin: *Read the file* under the *Fountain file* (`preview: "text"`) shows it with line numbers counted as *Check the Fountain* counts them, and `#L17` opens it at line 17. Editing it is still: change the file where it came from and attach it again.

**Every link to another guided setup takes the story along, and back (v194, needs Marvin #277).** The links an empty step shows and the lines at the end of a setup that open another setup now work as v191's *see also* links do: the other setup opens with what is chosen here and says at its top *You came from …, step N. ← Back to it*. *Bring the Fountain in: Narrate a story* (Takes of a line: Character, Chapter, Beat; Lines of a beat: Beat, Lines) opens Narrate a story on Story details with the story, its world and series. *Add a line: Lines of a beat* (Takes of a line, Line) opens on the beat's lines with the story and beat. *Add actors: Open call* and *Not in the troupe? Add an actor* (Casting call, Look through the troupe and The shortlist) open Open call's troupe with the world. The end of *Narrate a story* opens *Casting call* on The roles with the world and story; the end of *Open call* opens it on Story with the world.

**A beat's ambience layers over its scene's (v195, needs the runner rebuilt).** A beat's own **Ambience** no longer replaces its scene's bed: the scene's bed (the tavern) runs on under every beat of the scene without a break, and the beat's sound (a cheer, the fire) is a second bed laid over it for that beat, fading in and out around it. The beat's *Ambience level* and fades are for that bed; a beat with no sound of its own still sets the scene's bed for itself (the cabin going quiet for the letters). Naming the scene's own sound is the scene's bed, never two copies. The beat's bed is muffled as the scene's is (an INT. scene's is by default) unless its new *This beat's ambience heard* says **clear** (or **muffled**). The story's own Ambience is optional and separate: when the story names none, nothing plays outside the scenes' and beats' beds; if a bed runs under the whole film, the story's Ambience names it. In the manifest the beat's bed is `beat_ambience` beside the shot's `ambience` (the scene's); the audio cut lists it with `"over": "scene"`, and the script writes `SFX: crowd cheer, over the scene's ambience`. The Story Timeline still draws a beat's sound in place of its scene's until Marvin's timeline layers it too.

**The Story Timeline draws a beat's ambience over its scene's (v196, needs Marvin #278).** The Timeline's Ambience lane now shows what the render plays since v195. The scene's bed is one block across all its beats, and a beat's own sound is a second block for that beat, on a row below the scenes' beds and marked *over the scene*. It is muffled as the scene's bed is unless *This beat's ambience heard* says otherwise, and Play plays both together. The view reads that field through the lane's new `item_heard`.

**Make the script from the guided setup (v197).** *Narrate a story* has a step *Make the script*, after *Check what you have* and before *Clear what the import made* (still last). Its button, *Make the script (PDF)*, runs *Screenplay: make the script files of a story* for the production version: the PDF in Courier 12 to share or print, the Fountain, the web page and the breakdown, made from the records as they are now. Nothing makes it by itself, so press it again after a change. The button stays off until the import has made the beats (the story's Sheet log), and links to that step. When it is done, the job's line shows under the button and the PDF is the story's *Script (PDF)*. For *written* or *complete*, run the workflow under Automation -> Workflows.

**A world lists its stories, and every entry links up (v198).** A *World* entry's page has a *Stories* list (oldest first): the stories whose *World* field holds that world's slug, each a link. Going up, the field holding a parent's slug is a pick list with an open link: a story's *World* and *Series*, a series' *World*, and the *Story* of a chapter, scene, beat, line, character and sound (a beat's chapter and scene, a line's beat and a take's line already were). Applying the package adds the list and the pick lists to a workspace that has these types already.

**A picture you direct: `[[Pic: …]]` (v199).** A note `[[Pic: Close on the coin in her fist.]]` on a line of its own (also `[[Pic]]`, `[[Pic.]]`) starts a new picture as `===` does, and the import writes its words into the beat's **Shot direction** (`direction`), which the still's prompt reads: so the field the v183 note said would be filled by a later release is now filled from Pic notes (capitals shot lines still stay in the action). Other Fountain apps ignore it as a note. A Pic note with a `===` beside it is one break; right under (or above) a scene heading it directs the scene's first picture and makes no picture of the heading alone; with nothing after it, it makes none. A new beat gets the direction, an existing one only when its field is empty; one a person wrote is never written over. The beat's own Fountain text keeps the note; rewording only a Pic note is not a revision of the beat. The *Complete* script export writes each beat's Shot direction back as a Pic note above its picture, and the PDF never prints one. The check says, as worth a look only, when a Pic note is not read (inside a speech, mid-line, not closed on its line), follows another with no picture between (the first one's words are used), or is over 400 characters. See fountain.md, section 6: one picture per spoken sentence is now a thing to ask for, at the cost of one still each.

**Covers and stills from the world down (v200).** Every picture reads, widest first: the **world** (*Premise*, *Rules* and the new **Style reference**, e.g. its logo: sent last, for the painted style only), the **series** *Premise*, the **story** *Premise* (for mood) and *Setting*, the **chapter** *Summary*, then for a still the scene and beat as before. A still's prompt gains `WORLD:`, `SERIES:`, `STORY PREMISE (for mood only):` and `THIS CHAPTER:` before *SCENE* (`none` when empty). Stills and covers follow the story's *Picture shape*: tall (1024x1536), square (1024x1024) or landscape for wide-16x9 (1536x1024); none set, tall at the connection's size as before. **Screenplay: make a cover** (input `entry`, was `story`) takes a world, series, story or chapter and sets its *Cover image* (new on world, series and chapter): the world's *Rules* as its look, its *Style reference* as the style (else `style_cover`), leads optional (none: it shows the entry's new **What the cover shows**, else the premise), the gold title the only text. To use it: set the world's Style reference, Premise and Rules, give each story its World, run it on the entry. See marvin.md, stage 3.

**An info card and what stays changed travel in the Fountain (v201).** Two notes, each on a line of its own inside the picture it belongs to (never inside a speech), fill a beat as a Pic note fills its Shot direction: `[[Info card: label | title | sentence]]` (any slot may be empty; one slot alone is the title, `[[Info card: 2302]]`; two are the label and the title) and `[[Changes: …]]` (the beat's *Changes from here on*). They start and end no picture. An import fills them on a new beat, or on one whose field is empty, never over a value a person set (a beat with any of the card's three fields keeps its own card). One with no words, inline or inside a speech is only a note, and the check says so (worth a look: `info-card-unread`, `changes-unread`). The *complete* script writes both notes after the beat's `[[Beat NN. …]]` note, which no longer says *Info card:*, and none for a plain card (its words are centred as before). It also no longer writes a beat's heading as its action when the heading is all its picture says (a Pic note and its lines: `Exterior: Interstellar Space - 2302`, `!EXT. INTERSTELLAR SPACE - 2302`), which read back as words of the picture. A story with none of these exports byte for byte as before. See fountain.md, section 6. This file now starts at v180; v128 to v179 are in recent-older.md.

**The AI writes What the cover shows (v202).** *Screenplay: write the cover subject* (`screenplay-write-cover-subject`, inputs `entry`: a world, series, story or chapter, and `replace`: no or yes, default no) has the workspace AI write the entry's *What the cover shows* (`cover_subject`): one paragraph of at most about 60 words, the most striking drawable moment for the cover, from what the world, series, story and chapter say and what the entry's pictures show, with no invented people or events, no text in the picture, no people when its pictures show none, in the world's style words. It is saved on the entry to read and edit, and *Screenplay: make a cover* reads it. One already written is kept unless `replace` is yes: the run then asks you to close a note that shows both. Text only: no image and no image credit. See marvin.md, *Covers*.

**A person's actor sheet as the house sheets are (v203).** *Screenplay: make an actor's sheet* draws a person in one landscape row (1536x1024 by default): four full-length views, front, three-quarter, right-side profile, back, on plain cream, no text, where it used to ask for a tall 2 by 2 grid. A field left empty is said as *not specified* with a plain choice that differs from the style actor, never an empty slot; the style actor's sheet is for the finish only, with the words *do NOT copy that picture's person, face, hair, skin tone, clothes or colours*. A picture of the person is a third reference for the face, hair and build: the new optional input `source_picture`, else the actor's own picture (its General view, as the owner had uploaded for Mara: the new sheet keeps that person), else its first view at an age; the question says which is attached. The sheet becomes the actor's `general_view` only when you Keep it (it used to be attached before the question, so a skipped or denied one stayed). Open call's *Check the troupe* has four *Fix before making the sheet* lines (Age, Hair, Skin tone, Baseline wardrobe of a person actor); they block nothing, and *Make the sheet with AI* says that the actor's picture is the reference and nothing is replaced before Keep, and which of those fields is empty. The animal sheet is unchanged.

**The complete script drops nothing (v204).** *Make the script* in its complete version writes back every note the writer put in: the title page's own notes (on The Flash: Production, Visual style, Continuity, Timing) and each picture's (16 `[[Sentence: …]]` ids, 6 `[[Silent on purpose: …]]` reasons, any comment), in the same picture and order, from the story's and each beat's own Fountain text. A picture with a Pic note gets no `===` above it (The Flash had 21). Brought in again, the `[[Scene …]]` note fills the scene's place, light, set dressing and continuity and the `[[Beat NN. …]]` note a beat's focus and expression, new records or empty fields only; one not as the export writes it is only a note (`scene-note-unread`, `beat-note-unread`, worth a look). A story with no text of its own and no directions exports byte for byte as before. See fountain.md, section 6.

**Casting call: the master prompt has a step of its own (v205).** After *Write the master prompt*, which now shows the prompt it wrote (*The prompt the AI wrote*) and says its box of what is sent is to read only (change the costume or props on *Give the role to the actor*), come **The master prompt** (to read and edit) and **The master picture** (leave it empty to make the picture on the next step; upload one only if you already have a master), where there was one step with both. *Make the master picture* stays off until the role has an Age, and names the step to set it on, instead of failing with *no such tag(s): age-*. A fresh workspace has *Master prompt* outside the folded *Master picture* section of a Character; a workspace that has the type keeps its sections (the package never moves a field), so to see the prompt open there, take Master prompt out of its group once in the Character type. No other guided setup has a prompt and its picture in one step.

**A batch of stills or sounds asks once per item again (v206).** Marvin now runs a paid call (a picture, a sound effect) only in a run a person approved at one of its own Approval steps; a step passed by its `approved_when` is nobody's approval. So *Make the stills of a story* and *Make the sounds of a story*, whose one question used to stand for every item, made nothing: each take failed at once with "requires approval" and spent nothing. Now the one question queues them, and each still or sound asks for its own go-ahead in Approvals (Approve all answers them together); `spend_approved: yes` still means no Keep question for that item, so you keep them afterwards with *Keep the stills (sounds) that were made*, and it becomes `used` when the making starts. A single still or sound asks as before.

**A pick can show what is attached (v207; needs the Marvin change that adds `marks` to a pick).** In *Takes of a line*, the Line step shows a small "audio" label on every line that already has a take attached, so you can see which lines are done before you open one. The `status` still reads approved or draft; the label says the audio is there.

**A smoother camera and a wide film that stays wide (v208; needs a worker image rebuild).** The copy that goes to Marvin is now 720 on the short side in the film's own shape (a vertical Short 720x1280, a wide film 1280x720); it was a fixed 720x1280, which squashed a wide film into a vertical frame. A slow zoom or drift no longer jitters: the picture is now moved over four times the frame (it was twice), so each step is a quarter of a pixel instead of half, and the faint shimmer of the stills at rest on a Timeline play goes away. A 4-second clip takes about 0.25 seconds longer to make.

**Pictures that come before their words, tighter silences, nudged lines and a cleaner copy (v209; needs a worker image rebuild).** The render lays the sound out first and cuts the pictures from it. The story's new **Picture before the words** (`picture_lead`, seconds; a beat's own wins, 0 is at the first word) brings each beat's picture that long before its first word, while the line before is still being spoken (a silent beat's, that long before it starts): the voice runs on across the change on one continuous sound track, a dissolve (the story's `transition`/`transition_seconds`) is centred on the change, and a picture is never brought before 0 or closer than half a second after the one before. Empty: the picture comes where its beat's sound starts, as before, and every picture and sound is timed exactly as it was. **Lead-in / Gap / Tail of every beat** on the story (`line_lead`, `line_gap`, `line_tail`) replace the renderer's 0.3 / 0.25 / 0.35 s for every beat that does not set its own (e.g. 0.05 / 0.12 / 0.1 with a picture lead of 0.8 and a 0.8 s dissolve, the feel of the owner's mock D). A speech line's **Nudge** (`nudge`, seconds, may be negative) starts it later or earlier than it falls, so two voices can overlap (an interruption); the lines after it follow it, the beat lasts until its last line ends, captions follow, and a render, a filmstrip or a sound cut names any line laid wholly over another. In the Fountain it is `[[Nudge: -0.4]]` on its own line right before the speaker (or under the speaker's name); the import fills it on a new line or one with none, and the *complete* script writes it back. The beds, the music levels, the chapters and the filmstrip follow the pictures; sound effects keep their time from the beat's sound. The Timeline does not draw a picture lead, the story's silences or a nudge yet (a Marvin change). The copy for Marvin is now CRF 22 (was 28) with a 2.2 Mbit/s cap: on a slow move over a noisy test still it follows 0.63 of the motion where it followed 0.26, so the texture no longer freezes and jumps (the shimmer), and a 5-minute film stays under 86 MB; a draft's own encode is veryfast at CRF 23 (was ultrafast at 28).

**Scenes listed in the order they play (v210).** A Scene has a **Scene number** (`number`, text: 1, 2, 3; one put in later takes letters, 1a, 1b, as beats do), and the story's Scenes list is sorted by it, so a flashback scene made later sits where it plays, not at the bottom. The import numbers a new scene by where its place first appears in the script (a place seen again keeps its number) and gives a number to a scene that has none on a refresh; a number already set is never changed. The *complete* script writes each scene's own number in its heading (`#1a#`), else the count of places so far; it no longer takes a record with a number for a beat (a numbered scene came out as a forced heading with no `[[Scene …]]` note), so set scene numbers only on a worker of v210 or later. A workspace that has the Story type keeps its Scenes list sorted by creation (a package never changes a list's sort): set it to Number once in the Story type's related lists.

**Dual dialogue is written back and played together (v210; needs a worker image rebuild).** A line marked *Spoken at the same time as the line before* (`simultaneous`, from `NAME ^` in the Fountain) was written back without its `^` by the *complete* and *production* scripts, so the PDF and HTML showed the two speeches one after the other; both now write `NAME ^` (`NAME (V.O.) ^` with an extension) on the second speaker's cue, with the blank line the format puts between the two (screenplain sets them side by side only then). The render starts such a line when the line before it starts (its own nudge moves it from there), the next line follows the later of the two, the beat lasts until the later one ends, and both captions show at once. A line without the flag plays as before.

**Cues on a word: a sound, a picture or a camera move at the word it is written at (v211; needs a worker image rebuild).** A note among the words of a speech marks a word: `The hammer strikes [[Sound: clang | Camera: punch]] the wheel.`, `…launched in 1977, [[Pic: a small 1970s probe drifts in dark space, sepia]] had needed thirty-five years…` (valid Fountain: other apps ignore a note). The voice never reads it and the captions never show it. The import records each one on the beat as **Cues on words** (`cues`: `[{line: the speech line's slug, word: the words spoken before the note, sound, camera, pic}]`; a v3 tag such as `[sighs]` is not a word): a Sound note makes a draft Sound placeholder as a `[[Sound: …]]` note always did (nothing is made or paid for), numbered after the beat's own sounds and never also placed from the beat's start; a Camera note is kept (`{kind: punch, amount: 1.08}`) for v212, which moves the camera; a Pic note makes an **overlay beat** right after its beat (`04a`, `04b`: inserted numbers), with the note as its Shot direction, the same scene, **Comes in during a line** (`starts_during: {line, word}`) and a Least length of 0, so a Timeline draws it at no width. A note on its own line inside a speech is the same, and no longer splits the speech or goes unread (the check's `pic-unread` is now only for a Pic note inline in action). The render plays a cue's sound at the end of the word before the note and brings an overlay's picture in at the start of the word after it, dissolving over the beat's picture (the beat's or story's transition, else 0.6 s) while the voice runs on; the overlay adds no time, and a Least length keeps it up that long from the word. Times come from the take's word timings; a take without them is estimated from where the word stands in the text, and the render's log says which. A refresh gives the cues to a beat that has none and makes the overlay beats that are missing; a sound an earlier import placed from the beat's start is left there (the log says so). The *complete* script writes each note back among the words where it stood, and an overlay beat only as that note. The Timeline does not show cues yet.

**Camera cues on a word: punch, shake, nudge (v212; needs a worker image rebuild).** A Camera note among a line's words now moves the camera at the end of the word before it, on top of the beat's own move (and with a Sound in the same note, with that sound): `[[Camera: punch]]` a quick zoom in that settles back (6% in over 0.2 s, back over 0.3 s; `punch 1.1` is 10%), `[[Camera: shake]]` a short shake that dies away in 0.3 s (1.2% of the frame; `shake 2` is 2%), `[[Camera: nudge 0.7,0.4]]` a short drift toward that point of the picture (x, y from 0 to 1) with 4% more zoom to make room, which it then keeps. `[[Sound: clang | Camera: punch 1.08]]` does both. The moves are inside the same zoompan as the beat's move, on the four-times picture, so they stay smooth, and the frame never leaves the picture, at an edge or a clip's end. A cue that falls after an overlay picture came in moves that picture. Another word after `Camera:` is kept and written back, but does not move anything. The cues were already recorded by v211's import (`cues[].camera`), so nothing needs to be brought in again.

**A still with nobody in it says so, and its prompt reads as words (v213; the workflow reaches a workspace with the package card's Update; the import change needs a worker image rebuild).** *Screenplay: take a still* reads the beat's cast in two parts: the **characters** (a cast member with an actor who is not voice only and not marked `object`), with Role, Age, Costume, Props and Rules, and what is **in the picture but not a person** (one with no actor, such as The Flash's ships), with Role, Costume, Props and Rules and no Age. When the beat has no person in its cast, or no cast at all, the prompt ends with *NOBODY appears in this picture: no people, children or figures of any kind (the style picture's characters are not in it), unless THIS MOMENT names a figure*, so the world's style picture (the Storyworld logo's three children) no longer walks into a picture of ships. **THIS MOMENT** is the beat's picture without the `Delivery: Narrator (V.O.).` note the cut put after it (its `Narrator` asked for a figure), and a new import no longer writes that note into a beat's picture (each line keeps how it is said in its own fields; a refresh does not count an older picture's note as a difference). The prompt's lists read `Role:`, `Age:`, `Costume:`, `Props:`, `Rules:`, `Beat number:`, `Changes:` where they printed `data.role:` and the like. The cast lookups are a branch of the step that already looks up the world, series and chapter, so the workflow has one top-level step fewer (11).

**The Timeline draws the picture lead, the story's silences, nudged lines, dual dialogue and overlay beats (v214; needs Marvin #284; nothing to rebuild).** The *Timeline* view now names the timing keys Marvin #284 added, so it times a story as the render does since v209 to v211. Every beat's Lead-in / Gap / Tail come from the story's `line_lead` / `line_gap` / `line_tail` unless the beat sets its own. A beat's picture comes in its **Picture before the words** (`picture_lead`, the beat's else the story's) before its first word, over the end of the beat before, never closer than half a second after the picture before; the lines stay where they are, and the beds, overlay, camera, scene bands and chapters follow the pictures. A line's **Nudge** moves it and the lines after it, a line *spoken at the same time* starts with the line before (two rows when they overlap), and the beat lasts until its latest line ends. An **overlay beat** (`starts_during`, from a Pic note: 04a) is a narrow card with an accent edge where its picture comes in, at the word's start from the take's word timings (else from where the word stands in the text, which its tooltip says); it adds no time, as in the render. A line with no take is still estimated from its text (the render leaves it out, and plays an overlay on it as a beat of its own). Apply the package after Marvin #284 is deployed.

**Cues on words are edited as rows and drawn on the Timeline (v215; needs Marvin #287; nothing to rebuild).** The *Timeline* view declares the beat's **Cues on words** (`word_cues`: field `cues`, lane `lines`, sounds from `sounds`). A beat's page then edits them as rows, not JSON. Each row has one of the beat's lines, its words as chips (click a word, or the gap after it, to put the cue there; with a take's word timings the row says when it falls), a Sound (the same choice as the beat's Sounds, with its take to hear and a level), a Camera move (Punch, Shake or Nudge with its numbers) and a Picture (one of the story's overlay beats; when that beat comes in somewhere else, **Bring it in here** moves its *Comes in during a line* to the cue). It saves the same `cues` the import writes. The Timeline flags each cue on its line at the end of the word before it (♪ a sound, ✣ a camera move, ▣ a picture), and plays a cue's sound in the Sounds lane there, dimmed while the Sound has no take. A cue sound that runs past the beat's last line makes the beat longer, as in the render. Apply the package after Marvin #287 is deployed.

**A cue's sound and camera move can be nudged (v216; needs a worker image rebuild).** A row of a beat's **Cues on words** may carry `nudge` (seconds, may be negative), which the Timeline's Sounds lane sets when a cue's sound is dragged (Marvin side): the cue's sound and its camera move then come that much later (or earlier) than the word puts them (the end of the word before the note). A sound is never placed before the film starts (it may reach back into the beat before), and a camera move never before its own beat's picture is up; an overlay picture (a Pic cue) is placed by its own beat's *Comes in during a line* and is not moved. In the Fountain it is a part of the note beside a Sound or a Camera, `[[Sound: clang | Camera: punch | Nudge: 0.1]]` (seconds, an `s` allowed); the import writes it into the cue's row and the *complete* script writes it back. `[[Nudge: …]]` alone is still a line's own nudge (v209).
